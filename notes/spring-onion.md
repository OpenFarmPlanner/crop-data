## Short Description
- Spring onion / bunching onion (`Lauchzwiebel`, `Frühlingszwiebel`) is grown
  for its mild-tasting green shaft and leaves; unlike storage onions it does
  not form a pronounced round bulb.
- Botanical identity note: the modern German-market "Lauchzwiebel" category is
  dominated by `Allium fistulosum` (Winterheckenzwiebel/Welsh onion), which
  forms clumping, multi-shoot tufts rather than a single bulb. Historically
  (before the 1980s in Germany) the same market category was filled by
  bunching-type `Allium cepa` varieties such as `White Lisbon`, which form a
  single plant per bulb rather than a tuft. Both species are still sold under
  the "Lauchzwiebel"/"spring onion" name today, so the species cannot be
  assumed from the market name alone; it must be verified per variety (see
  `spring-onion-baja-verde.md` for a case where this could not be fully
  resolved).
- Hardy, frost-tolerant annual/short-lived perennial; low to moderate nutrient
  demand; needs even moisture, especially while shafts are filling out.
- Growth duration: source ranges conflict (see `Research Status`); left open
  at the general-crop level rather than stated as one falsely precise figure.

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow into a fine, loose seedbed; germinates in 14-28 days
  at 15-20°C. Sow in successions for a continuous harvest; late-summer/autumn
  sowings of frost-hardy types can overwinter for an early spring harvest.
- Sowing: March to July, plus an autumn sowing as an overwintering crop where
  a frost-hardy type is used.
- Sowing depth: about 1-2 cm.

- Spacing: about 30-40 cm between rows; thin to roughly 2.5 cm within the row
  for slender shafts (wider spacing gives thicker shafts).
- Site: full sun, loose, well-drained, humus-rich soil; heavy clay or very
  sandy soils are unsuitable.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest by pulling or cutting once shafts reach usable size; `Allium
  fistulosum` types regrow from an intact growing point after cutting,
  allowing repeat harvests from the same planting.
- Used fresh, raw or lightly cooked, in salads and Asian-style dishes.

## Notes
- Because the "Lauchzwiebel" category can span two botanical species with
  different growth habits (clumping/multi-shoot `fistulosum` types vs.
  single-shoot `cepa` types), variety notes must confirm and record the
  species where possible rather than inheriting this general note's
  `fistulosum` framing by default.

## Research Status
- Researched on: 2026-09-18
- General crop note exists: not applicable (this is the general crop note)
- Open source conflicts: growth duration from sowing to harvest is given
  inconsistently across sources (about 6-8 weeks by one source, 8-12 weeks by
  another, "two to three months after germination" by a third); no single
  reliable figure was found, so growth duration is left open at the general
  level rather than forced into one number.
- Open mapping questions: whether the general "Lauchzwiebel"/spring-onion
  crop record should represent `Allium fistulosum` only, or needs a documented
  split for `Allium cepa`-type bunching onions, is unresolved; flagged here so
  it is not silently decided at variety level.
- Public-readiness: needs review; growth duration and species scope are open.
