## Short Description
- `Rougella` (RZ F1) is a firm, round, uniformly red "intermediate type"
  slicing tomato bred by Rijk Zwaan, explicitly marketed as a `Salattomate`
  by at least one seed/plant retailer
  ([bio-jungpflanzen.com](https://www.bio-jungpflanzen.com/Bio-Tomatenpflanze-Rougella-im-7x7-cm-Erdballen/50403-7GE):
  "veredelte Salattomate"). This confirms the task's tentative `Salattomate`
  guess for Rougella with medium confidence (see Research Status).
- Growth habit: suitable for both loose-fruit and truss (cluster) harvest
  ([Rijk Zwaan](https://www.rijkzwaan.de/tomate/ROUGELLA-F1-prdSL11023-ctgCrops.tomato)),
  which is the truss/Strauchtomate cultivation-type note referenced in the
  general `Salattomate` crop note rather than a separate Kultur.
- Fruit weight: about 100-115 g; very early and uniform ripening; glossy,
  intensely red, firm fruit described as having "gardener resistance" - i.e.
  fruit holds well on the plant if picking is delayed.
- F1 hybrid; commonly sold as grafted ("veredelt") young plants.
- Growth duration: no concrete days-to-harvest figure found in the sources
  reviewed; left open, see Comparison below.

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: Suitable for lightly heated greenhouse cultivation on
  rockwool or in soil, plantable from March in protected structures;
  outdoor planting after the last frost.
- Sowing: 02-01 to 04-15 (reused from the general `Salattomate` window; no
  Rougella-specific sowing date was published in the sources reviewed)
- Outdoor planting: from mid-May outdoors; from March under protection

- Spacing: no Rougella-specific figure found; general `Salattomate` range of
  60-90 cm rows x 45-70 cm in-row reused
- Site: sunny, warm; suited to both greenhouse/tunnel and open-field
  cultivation

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest either as loose single fruit or as a whole truss/cluster.
- Fresh eating and slicing; firm flesh and good shelf life on the plant are
  highlighted by the sources.

## Notes
- Described as forgiving of a delayed harvest ("gardener resistance"),
  useful for home gardeners who cannot pick daily.
