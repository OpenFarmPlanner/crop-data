## Short Description
- Endivie (Kultur name; English name Endive; see "Identity And Mapping Note"
  for why the ambiguous English name is used anyway) is `Cichorium endivia`,
  a cool-season leaf chicory grown for summer to late-autumn harvest. Two leaf
  types exist, smooth/broad-leaved (`Eskariol`/`Escariol`, `Cichorium endivia`
  var. `latifolium`) and curly/finely divided (`Frisée`, var. `crispum`).
  This note merges what were briefly two separate Kulturen ("Glatte Endivie"
  and "Krause Endivie") back into one; see "Identity And Mapping Note" for
  why.
- Medium nutrient demand (Mittelzehrer); even moisture; sensitive to bolting
  under cold spells combined with long days, and under sustained heat above
  about 25-28 °C.
- Growth duration: 60 days from transplanting to harvest (planning value,
  averaged across both leaf types; see "Planning Value Derivations").
- Harvest window: 18 days per planting (planning value, averaged).
- Propagation duration: 27 days (pre-cultivation, sowing to transplanting;
  planning value, averaged).
- Expected yield: 4.75 kg/m² (planning value, averaged; see "Planning Value
  Derivations" for why this figure is uncertain).

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: pre-cultivate in trays or pressed-soil blocks, one seed per
  cell/pot, kept warm (germinates best around 16-20 °C; a cold shock during
  young-plant growth can trigger bolting). Sow shallow; the seed germinates
  poorly in warm soil above about 20 °C.
- Sowing: 2026-05-16 to 2026-07-17 (averaged main-crop window; the two leaf
  types were separately sourced at early June-late July and early May-mid
  July, see "Planning Value Derivations").
- Outdoor planting: 3-5 weeks after sowing (range covers both leaf types'
  sourced values of 3-4 and 3-5 weeks).

### Direct Sowing
- Description: mentioned by seed companies for both leaf types (higher seed
  quantity than transplants), but pre-cultivation is treated as the standard
  method in the sources reviewed; direct sowing is not the planning basis
  here.
- Sowing depth: about 1 cm (both leaf types agree; some garden sources give
  2-3 cm).

- Spacing: about 33 cm between rows, 30 cm within the row (about 10
  plants/m²; averaged, see "Planning Value Derivations").
- Site: full sun to partial shade, humus- and nutrient-rich soil with even
  moisture, ideally sandy-loam to gravelly-loam, neutral to slightly alkaline
  pH of about 6.5-7. Avoid excess nitrogen, which is linked to crown rot and
  leaf-edge burn in different sources. Mature plants tolerate some frost.

## Harvest & Use
- Cut whole heads/rosettes once filled. Smooth-leaved heads are often
  blanched or self-blanch with age to reduce bitterness; curly-leaved
  rosettes are also commonly blanched to enlarge the pale heart.
- Use: raw in salads, also lightly cooked or in stews. Curly-leaved heads
  have a shorter post-harvest shelf life than smooth-leaved heads (sourced
  difference, see "Differences By Leaf Type").

## Notes
- Rotation: avoid following other chicory-family crops (radicchio, chicory,
  witloof); a break of 3-4 years on the same bed is recommended.
- Crown rot and leaf-edge burn in dense, wet heads are typical problems; wet
  weather and high nitrogen increase both.

## Differences By Leaf Type
Structured planning values above are averaged across both leaf types (see
"Identity And Mapping Note" for why). This section keeps the sourced
qualitative and quantitative differences that were found between the smooth
(`Eskariol`/Escariol) and curly (`Frisée`) type, so they are not lost by the
merge.
- Taste and texture: curly type is finer, more delicate and salad-like;
  smooth type is described as milder to more bitter depending on the source,
  and forms a denser, more compact head.
- Storage: curly type keeps only a few days after harvest; smooth type keeps
  longer in the field and after harvest
  ([Gemüsekorb](https://www.gemuesekorb.info/salate/endivie.php),
  [Samen.de](https://samen.de/blog/unterschiede-zwischen-eskariol-und-frisee-welche-endivienart-passt-zu-ihrem-garten.html)).
- Frost tolerance: sources directly contradict each other on which type is
  hardier. [Samen.de](https://samen.de/blog/unterschiede-zwischen-eskariol-und-frisee-welche-endivienart-passt-zu-ihrem-garten.html)
  states curly types tolerate about -3 °C and smooth types about -1 °C;
  [Sperli](https://www.sperli.de/anbauexperte/kulturanleitung-endivien/) and
  [Kiepenkerl](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/endivien/) state
  the opposite (smooth type very frost tolerant, to about -5 °C by one
  source). No official trial source (LfL, Bio Austria, Agroscope) was found
  to settle this. Left open deliberately; do not pick a side.
- Yield and nitrogen requirement: the only structured, disputed figures.
  [LKSH's published DüV Annex 4 Table 4](https://www.lksh.de/fileadmin/PDFs/Landwirtschaft/Duengung/Stickstoffbedarfswertefuer_Gemuesekulturen_und_Erdbeeren.pdf)
  (a German fertilization-regulation compliance table, verified directly
  against the source PDF) lists smooth-leaved endive at a yield level of 600
  dt/ha (6 kg/m²) and 190 kg N/ha, versus curly/Frisée at 350 dt/ha (3.5
  kg/m²) and 150 kg N/ha. This is the strongest single source for a real
  yield gap between the types. However, it was not corroborated elsewhere:
  independent German ([gemueseliebe.de](https://gemueseliebe.de)), Belgian
  ([BioCérès](https://www.bioceres.be/fiche/la-chicoree-frisee-et-scarole))
  and French ([chefsimon.com](https://chefsimon.com/articles/pratique-proportions-et-grammages-des-legumes))
  sources each describe both leaf types with one shared cultivation protocol,
  cycle length, or serving weight, and distinguish them only qualitatively
  (leaf shape, texture, ease of cultivation, winter suitability), not by
  yield or head size. A DüV table reflects a regulatory nitrogen-planning
  assumption tied to an assumed yield level for compliance purposes, not a
  measured yield trial, which may explain the gap without it being a fixed
  botanical ceiling (for example, it could reflect typical market form:
  smooth types are commonly grown to a fuller, denser head, curly types often
  harvested as a looser rosette). Because the evidence is genuinely mixed,
  the merged general value averages the two DüV figures rather than picking
  one side (see "Planning Value Derivations"); this is a documented,
  deliberate averaging choice, not a resolved fact.
- Bolting and early planting: one source
  ([Kiepenkerl](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/endivien/))
  recommends curly types for plantings before mid-June because they are
  described as less cold-sensitive and less bolting-prone early in the
  season; smooth types are then recommended for later/main-season plantings.
  This is folded into the averaged sowing window above rather than kept as
  two separate windows.

## Identity And Mapping Note
- Merge decision (2026-09-28): this crop was briefly split on 2026-09-24 into
  two Kulturen, "Glatte Endivie" (Escariol/Eskariol) and "Krause Endivie"
  (Frisée), under the "lowest cultivation level" naming rule, based on
  apparent differences in yield, nitrogen demand, growth duration, harvest
  window and spacing (see the former `escarole.md` and `frisee.md` notes in
  git history, and [the original split migration
  note](../docs/endive-split-migration.md), now marked superseded).
  On 2026-09-28, that split was reconsidered and reversed after further
  research (documented here and in [the merge migration
  note](../docs/endive-merge-2026-09-28.md)) found that most of the
  differences underlying the split do not hold up:
  - Growth duration and harvest window differences (70 vs 49 days; 21 vs 14
    days) rested on choosing different source ranges per type rather than on
    an independently measured difference; both types' own sources
    (`Samen.de` for growth duration; French/Belgian production guides found
    in the 2026-09-28 research) describe a single shared cycle length for
    both leaf types.
  - Spacing differences (35x30 cm vs 30x30 cm) rest on the same kind of
    source-range spread, not a confirmed type-specific requirement.
  - Only the yield/nitrogen-demand gap (DüV table) has a distinct, named-by-
    type source, and even that was not corroborated by other production
    guides (see "Differences By Leaf Type" above).
  - Given this, keeping two Kulturen for a difference that is mostly a
    modeling artifact contradicts the same "narrowest reusable crop identity"
    principle that justified the original split: the split values were not
    reliably type-specific either.
  This reversal is a deliberate, documented decision, not a silent
  correction; the original split reasoning, the counter-research, and the
  final averaging methodology are all kept in the notes rather than erased,
  per the "Explicit, Not Silent" project rule.
- Naming: bare "Endive" is ambiguous in US English (it usually means Belgian
  endive/chicory, `Cichorium intybus`). It is used here anyway because this
  is the pre-existing linked official OpenFarmPlanner crop species name (see
  [the merge migration note](../docs/endive-merge-2026-09-28.md)), and
  because "Endiviensalat" is the German umbrella term that both prior
  sub-Kulturen already used as informal shorthand. The German Kultur name is
  "Endivie".
- Synonyms/aliases: `Eskariol`, `Escariol`, `Frisée`, `Friséesalat`,
  `Endiviensalat`, `Winterendivie`, `Endivie glatt`, `Endivie krause`, `Curly
  endive`.
- Botanical naming: [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Endivien_Erwerbsanbau)
  uses `Cichorium endivia var. latifolium` (smooth) and `var. crispum`
  (curly). A search summary of [Plants of the World
  Online](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:194527-1)
  suggested `var. crispum` is the accepted variety-level name with
  `var. latifolium` as a separate variety; [Pl@ntUse](https://plantuse.plantnet.org/en/Cichorium_endivia_Latifolium_Group_(Common_names))
  treats the smooth type as a cultivar group instead. The POWO page could not
  be opened directly in either research pass (access blocked), so the
  accepted rank is unverified. Not resolved; kept as an open mapping
  question, informational only since the merge no longer needs a rank
  decision to separate Kulturen.
- Variety: `Anconi` (smooth/Eskariol type) is documented in
  [endive-anconi.md](endive-anconi.md) and now compares against this merged
  general note.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Averaging methodology: for every quantitative planning value where the two
  former leaf-type notes (`escarole.md` growth 70d, harvest window 21d,
  propagation 25d, spacing 35x30cm, yield 6kg/m²; `frisee.md` growth 49d,
  harvest window 14d, propagation 28d, spacing 30x30cm, yield 3.5kg/m²) had
  different values, the merged value is the arithmetic mean of the two,
  rounded half up to the nearest whole day/cm where the field needs an
  integer (per "Respect Field And Form Constraints"). This is a documented
  averaging choice made on 2026-09-28, not a re-derivation from primary
  sources; the original per-type source basis for each number is kept in git
  history in the former `escarole.md`/`frisee.md` notes and summarized in
  [the merge migration note](../docs/endive-merge-2026-09-28.md).
  - Growth duration: (70 + 49) / 2 = 59.5, rounded to 60 days.
  - Propagation duration: (25 + 28) / 2 = 26.5, rounded to 27 days.
  - Harvest window: (21 + 14) / 2 = 17.5, rounded to 18 days.
  - Row spacing: (35 + 30) / 2 = 32.5, rounded to 33 cm; within-row spacing
    was 30 cm for both types, unchanged.
  - Yield: (6 + 3.5) / 2 = 4.75 kg/m², kept unrounded (one decimal place is
    already the convention used by the source figures; no integer
    field/form constraint is documented for this field). This average
    combines a DüV-table-derived planning yield level for each leaf type;
    see "Differences By Leaf Type" for why the underlying gap is itself
    disputed. Treat 4.75 kg/m² as a rough planning midpoint, not a
    source-backed figure.
  - Sowing window: start date is the midpoint of 2026-05-01 (curly) and
    2026-06-01 (smooth) = 2026-05-16; end date is the midpoint of
    2026-07-15 (curly) and 2026-07-20 (smooth) = 2026-07-17. Averaging
    calendar dates like this is a simplification; it does not necessarily
    match either type's optimal window on its own. Flagged as an open
    mapping question below.
  - Outdoor planting timing, sowing depth, thousand kernel weight (1.5 g) and
    nutrient demand (medium) were already equal or overlapping between the
    two former notes and needed no averaging.
- Field/form constraint: whole integer days for durations; cm for spacing.
  No documented integer constraint found for the yield field.
- Thousand kernel weight: 1.5 g, unchanged from both former notes (Hortipendium
  1.3-1.6 g general; ReinSaat 1.3-2 g for smooth types, 1.42-1.67 g for named
  curly varieties).
- Nutrient demand: medium (Mittelzehrer), unchanged; both leaf types' DüV
  nitrogen figures (150 and 190 kg N/ha) sit in the same "medium" band as
  other medium feeders in the same table (head lettuce, leaf parsley),
  confirmed by [BUND Region Hannover](https://bund-region-hannover.de/fileadmin/hannover/BUND_aktiv/Universum_Kleingarten/Publikationen_zum_Download/Handreichung_Stark-_Mittel-_und_Schwachzehrer.pdf),
  which lists "Endivie" as Mittelzehrer without separating leaf types.

  see git history), 2026-09-28 (counter-research and merge back into one
  Kultur)
- General crop note exists: not applicable (this is the general crop note)
- Open source conflicts:
  - Frost tolerance: sources directly contradict each other on which leaf
    type is hardier (see "Differences By Leaf Type"); not resolved.
  - Yield/nitrogen demand: DüV table shows a clear per-type gap; other
    production guides describe both types with shared, undifferentiated
    figures. Resolved here by averaging, documented as a compromise, not a
    finding.
  - Growth duration, harvest window and spacing: different sources give
    different ranges per type; treated as source-range noise, not a
    confirmed botanical difference, hence merged/averaged.
- Open mapping questions:
  - The averaged sowing window (2026-05-16 to 2026-07-17) is a date-midpoint
    simplification and may not suit either leaf type's actual optimum;
    revisit if field feedback or a better source is found.
  - Botanical rank of `var. latifolium` vs `var. crispum` (cultivar group or
    formal variety) is unverified (POWO page not accessible).
  - Whether OpenFarmPlanner should re-split this Kultur later if better
    type-specific yield evidence turns up is an open question; the 2026-09-24
    split and its full sourced comparison remain in git history and in the
