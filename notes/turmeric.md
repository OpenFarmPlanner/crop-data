## Short Description
- Turmeric (`Kurkuma`, `Curcuma longa`) is a tropical rhizome crop of the
  ginger family (Zingiberaceae), grown in Central Europe in greenhouse or foil
  tunnel; separate from `ginger.md` because it has a different species and
  harvest use (yellow-fleshed rhizome).
- Needs even warmer conditions than ginger; typical range 20-30 °C, night
  temperatures should not fall below about 15 °C outdoors
  ([Plantura](https://www.plantura.garden/gemuese/kurkuma/kurkuma-pflanzenportrait)).
- Growth duration: 170 days from planting out to first harvest, propagation
  140 days, harvest window 30 days; planning values for an unheated Central
  European tunnel with an early start (see `Planning Value Derivations`).
  Total about 10 months, as reported by an Austrian organic producer. Tropical
  field values (7-9 months from planting) are a different regime.

## Template Adaptation
- Same as ginger (`ginger.md`): rhizome pieces instead of seed. The

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: divide the rhizome into pieces of about 3-5 cm and let the cut
  surfaces dry for a few days; pre-sprout warm on a heat mat
  ([kraut&rüben](https://www.krautundrueben.de/kurkuma-die-wunderwurzel-anbauen-und-ernten-3398)).
  Tropical practice also uses treated mother rhizome pieces, see
  [Indian Spices Board](https://www.indianspices.com/sites/default/files/cultivation_practices-TURMERIC-1.pdf).
- Start: December to January in the commercial Austrian tunnel example
  ([Ja! Natuerlich](https://www.janatuerlich.at/magazin/exotisch-und-aus-oesterreich-bio-kurkuma/));
  a start in March (garden sources) is too late for full maturity, see
  `Planning Value Derivations`.
- Planting out: end of April into an unheated tunnel in that Austrian example;
  outdoors in pots only after mid-May and keep warm.
- Planting depth: about 5 cm (garden-level source only; no extension value
  found).
- Spacing: no Central European tunnel spacing found; left open. Tropical
  field reference only: 25 x 30 cm or 30 x 15 cm on ridges or beds
  ([Indian Spices Board](https://www.indianspices.com/sites/default/files/cultivation_practices-TURMERIC-1.pdf)).
- Site: brightest, warmest position, permeable substrate, evenly moist.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest in October when leaves yellow and die back and tunnel temperatures
  stay below about 18-20 °C for a longer period, before cold (Austrian
  example above). Alternatively overwinter dry at about 10 °C to regrow.
- Tropical field (India): harvest 7-9 months after planting, generally
  January to March; not transferable to Central Europe.
- Used fresh or dried and ground as a spice.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Definitions: propagation duration is the start of the rhizome pieces until
  planting out; growth duration is counted from planting out to first harvest
  (as in `ginger.md`).
- Two regimes must not be mixed. Tropical field: the Indian Spices Board gives
  7-9 months from planting (a short 180-day variety exists) and about
  38 t/ha fresh rhizomes for the variety IISR Pragati, i.e. about 3.8 kg/m²
  (38,000 kg / 10,000 m²). Central European unheated tunnel: only the
  Austrian producer report below.
- Growth duration: 170 days. Planting out at the end of April and harvest in
  mid-October gives about 170 days. This is derived from one producer report
  ([Ja! Natuerlich](https://www.janatuerlich.at/magazin/exotisch-und-aus-oesterreich-bio-kurkuma/))
  and is shorter than the tropical 210-270 days; it works only with the long
  early propagation phase. Inferred, not a direct source value. Earlier
  value was 210.
- Propagation duration: 140 days. Start in December and planting out at the
  end of April gives about 140-150 days (from early January about 120).
  Inferred from the two dates; no source gives a day count. Earlier value
  was 56.
- Total of 310 days matches the roughly 10 months of the producer report and
  the garden sources' 7-10 months only at the upper end.
- Harvest window: 30 days, inferred; harvest from October when leaves yellow
  and die back, before cold. No source gives a day count.
- Start date conflict: December/January (Austrian commercial example) versus
  March (garden sources). Because the tunnel season from end of April to
  October gives only about 170 days, and tropical crops need 7-9 months in
  total, the December/January start is preferred for the crop calendar. A
  March start is kept as a lower-yield option and not modeled.
- Yield: kg/m² left open. The Austrian report gives about 0.75 kg per plant
  (about 8,000 plants in 2021) but no planting density, so a conversion
  would be falsely precise. The Indian field figure is not transferable.
- Planting depth: only the garden value of 5 cm; extension-level depth
  recommendations for Central Europe were not found.
- Field/form constraint: whole days.
- Uncertainty: large; based on one commercial example; outdoors in pots the
  season is much shorter and may not reach maturity in Central Europe.
