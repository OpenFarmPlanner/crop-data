## Short Description
- `Soßentomate` (sauce/paste tomato) is `Solanum lycopersicum`. It covers
  elongated, oval to plum- or bottle-shaped fruit with relatively low water
  content and few seeds, bred for cooking down into sauce, paste, or
  preserves rather than for fresh slicing. This includes both Roma-type and
  San-Marzano-type fruit shapes, which are documented as a type distinction
  inside this one Kultur rather than as separate Kulturen (see the variety
  notes for `Bolstar` and `Monterrey`).
- `Tomate`/`Tomato` alone is a generic culinary umbrella term for
  `Solanum lycopersicum`, not a valid Kultur value in this project. Siblings:
  [`Fleischtomate`](beefsteak-tomato.md) (beefsteak/oxheart, large slicing
  fruit), `Soßentomate` (this note), [`Kirschtomate`](cherry-tomato.md)
  (cherry, small sweet fruit), and [`Salattomate`](salad-tomato.md) (round
  salad/slicing fruit). Other fruit-size classes such as `Cocktailtomate`
  may exist botanically but are not documented in this batch of notes.
  Confirmed via [ReinSaat](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/EN/tomaten_paradeiser/),
  which files Roma-type and San-Marzano-type varieties under Sauce tomatoes.
- Indeterminate, staked/trellised growth habit typical for garden and
  greenhouse varieties; determinate/bush paste types also exist in the wider
  market but are not covered by the varieties in this batch.
- Growth duration: left open here; the Roma-type and San-Marzano-type
  varieties researched so far give sowing/planting windows but no concrete
  days-to-harvest figures, so a general value would be falsely precise.

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: Start indoors, plant out after the last frost. Staking
  needed for indeterminate sauce-tomato varieties.
- Sowing: 02-15 to 03-31
- Outdoor planting: from mid-May, after hardening off

- Spacing: 70-100 cm between rows; 35-60 cm within the row (wide general
  range reflecting a source conflict; see below)
- Site: full sun, warm and sheltered, ideally with some rain protection
  (tunnel, greenhouse, or south-facing wall) since paste tomatoes are prone
  to fruit cracking and fungal disease in wet conditions

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest when fully colored and firm; fruit is typically picked loose
  rather than on the truss for processing.
- Used for sauce, paste, passata, and preserving; less suited to fresh
  slicing due to firmer, lower-water flesh.

## Notes
- Benefits from rain-sheltered cultivation (tunnel/greenhouse) to reduce
  cracking and blight pressure, a recurring theme in the sources for both
  Roma-type and San-Marzano-type fruit.
- Long fruit clusters (trusses of 7-12 fruits) are typical for this class.
