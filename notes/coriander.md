## Short Description
- Coriander / cilantro (`Koriander`, leaf use `Blattkoriander`) is
  `Coriandrum sativum`, here treated as the leaf crop rather than the seed
  spice crop.
- Fast annual, bolts in heat and long days. Low to moderate nutrient demand,
  even moisture. Best sown in successions.
- Growth duration to a first leaf cut: about 4-5 weeks.

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow in rows; coriander has a taproot and dislikes
  transplanting. Repeat sowings every about 2 weeks.
- Sowing: about late April to July outdoors; earlier and later under
  protection.
- Sowing depth: 1-2 cm.

- Spacing: about 20-25 cm between rows, dense within the row for cut leaf.
- Site: sun to light shade; light, free-draining soil; cooler conditions delay
  bolting.

## Harvest & Use
- Cut leaves from about 4-5 weeks after sowing, always before flowering.
  Cut-and-come-again is possible for a few weeks until the plant bolts.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: 30 days as a planning value for a first leaf cut. Garden
  sources give about 4-5 weeks (roughly 28-35 days) to leaf harvest
  ([meine-ernte](https://www.meine-ernte.de/pflanzen-a-z/kraeuter/koriander/),
  [Plantura](https://www.plantura.garden/kraeuter/koriander/koriander-ernten)).
  30 days is a whole-day value near the lower end for a first cut. The live
  variety record `Blattkoriander Thai` stores 35 days.
- Harvest window: 60 days as an inferred planning value for repeated cutting
  before the plant bolts; strongly weather dependent and shorter in heat.
  Documented as inferred. The live variety record stores 75 days.
- Yield: left open. No reliable leaf-yield source was found for coriander; herb
  leaf yield per m² depends heavily on cutting frequency and season.
- Harvest method: left open until a yield basis exists.
- Propagation duration: left empty. Coriander is direct sown.
