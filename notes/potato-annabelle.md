## Short Description
- `Annabelle` is a very early ("sehr früh"), firm-cooking ("festkochend")
  potato (`Solanum tuberosum`) with long-oval tubers, pale yellow flesh, and
  a thin, delicate yellow skin; bred from Nicola x Monalisa and registered in
  the Netherlands in 2002
  ([eat.de - Kartoffel Annabelle](https://eat.de/magazin/kartoffel-annabelle-eigenschaften-und-verwendung/),
  [Wikipedia - Annabelle (Kartoffel)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annabelle_(Kartoffel))).
- Fine, tender, slightly waxy flavor with an intense aroma; used as a salt,
  salad, pan-fried, or steamed potato, and for gratins
  ([eat.de - Kartoffel Annabelle](https://eat.de/magazin/kartoffel-annabelle-eigenschaften-und-verwendung/)).
- Maturity group: very early. Growth duration is given as about 90-110 days
  from planting in one source and about 80-90 days in another; both are
  documented rather than silently resolved, and both fall within the "very
  early" 90-110 day range from the general `Kartoffel` note's Reifegruppen
  overview (the 80-90 day figure is slightly below even that range).
- Yield: no kg/m² or kg/plant figure was found in the sources checked.

## Sowing & Planting

### Seed-Tuber Planting
- Description: plant seed tubers like other very-early potatoes; as one of
  the earliest varieties, it is commonly pre-sprouted to advance harvest
  further, consistent with the general `Kartoffel` note's pre-sprouting
  guidance for early varieties.
- Planting: March to May
  ([eat.de - Kartoffel Annabelle](https://eat.de/magazin/kartoffel-annabelle-eigenschaften-und-verwendung/)).
- Planting depth: reused from the general `Kartoffel` note (6-10 cm, with a
  10-15 cm alternative documented as an open conflict there); no
  `Annabelle`-specific figure was found.

- Spacing: reused from the general `Kartoffel` note (about 30-40 cm within
  the row, 60-80 cm between rows); no `Annabelle`-specific figure was found.
- Site: full sun, loose, well-drained, nutrient-rich soil, matching the
  general `Kartoffel` crop's requirements.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest from June, with the overall harvest period extending June to
  September depending on planting time and weather
  ([eat.de - Kartoffel Annabelle](https://eat.de/magazin/kartoffel-annabelle-eigenschaften-und-verwendung/)).
- As one of the earliest varieties, it clears the bed early enough to allow a
  follow-on crop such as kale or Chinese cabbage in the same season.
- Used as a firm-cooking early potato: salt/steamed potatoes, potato salad,
  pan-fried potatoes, gratin.

## Notes
- Crop rotation: avoid growing after other Solanaceae on the same spot for
  several years, as documented in the general `Kartoffel` note.
- Well established on the German market as one of the most popular
  early-potato varieties.

## Comparison With General Crop Data
- Maturity group / growth duration: `Annabelle` is very early, matching the
  general `Kartoffel` note's "very early: 90-110 days" Reifegruppe reference
  most closely. One source's 80-90 day figure is somewhat below even that
  range; this is documented as an open source conflict rather than silently
  picking either figure or forcing it to match the general note's range.
  This is the key variety-specific difference from the general crop's
  intentionally open growth-duration value: a plausible variety-specific
  range now exists, even though it is not a single settled figure.
- Planting: `Annabelle`'s March-to-May planting window starts earlier than
  the general `Kartoffel` note's "early April to late May" window; consistent
  with it being one of the earliest varieties and commonly pre-sprouted or
  planted for an early crop. Documented as a real variety-specific difference
  reflecting the maturity group, not a contradiction of the general note.
- Spacing and planting depth: no `Annabelle`-specific source was found, so
  the general `Kartoffel` note's values are intentionally reused. No
  variety-specific source contradicts them.
- Yield: the general `Kartoffel` note leaves yield open. No `Annabelle`-
  specific kg/m² or kg/plant figure was found, so no yield value is set here
  either; this is an intentional reuse of the general note's open state.
