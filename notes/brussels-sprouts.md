## Short Description
- Brussels sprouts (`Rosenkohl`, `Kohlsprossen`) are `Brassica oleracea var.
  gemmifera`, grown for the small axillary buds ("sprouts") along a tall stem.
- Biennial grown as an annual; one of the longest-season brassica crops, with
  a full cycle commonly described as up to 9 months from sowing to final
  harvest. Frost tolerant; flavor is often considered improved after frost.
- Growth duration differs strongly by type: early varieties are ready in
  roughly 6-7 months, late/very frost-hardy varieties need the full 9 months.
  No single general value is set here (see `Planning Value Derivations`).

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: pre-cultivate for an even stand; standard method for this
  long-season crop.
- Sowing: from about March under protection, or outdoors on a nursery bed from
  the second week of April to early May.
- Outdoor planting: about 6-8 weeks after sowing (typically around the second
  week of June).

- Spacing: about 50-60 cm between rows, 50-60 cm within the row (roughly
  3-4 plants/m²); some sources give a tighter 30-50 cm in-row spacing for
  smaller/earlier types.
- Site: full sun; firm, nutrient-rich, well-drained soil with steady moisture.
  High nutrient demand. Staking or earthing up may be needed on exposed sites
  for this tall crop. Net cover against cabbage fly, cabbage white, and aphids
  is useful.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest sprouts progressively from the bottom of the stem upward as they
  reach usable size, typically from autumn through winter; pinching out the
  growing tip can encourage more even sprout sizing.
- Eaten cooked.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: left open as a single general value. General garden sources
  describe a season of up to 9 months, with early varieties at about 6-7
  months and late, frost-hardy varieties needing the full 9 months
  ([Wurzelwerk](https://www.wurzelwerk.net/gemuesegarten/gemueseportraits/rosenkohl/)-style
  guidance). Because the project's variety `Nautic` is itself documented by
  its breeder with a specific day count, and the type spread is large, this
  general note leaves growth duration open rather than averaging it; the
  variety note carries the concrete value.
- Yield: about 0.6-0.8 kg/m² (60-80 kg per 100 m²) is given by general garden
  sources as a home-garden planning range; kept as a range rather than a
  single value pending a more authoritative source.
