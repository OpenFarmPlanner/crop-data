## Short Description
- Spinach (`Spinat`) is `Spinacia oleracea`, a fast cool-season leafy crop for
  spring and autumn/winter harvest. Bolts quickly in long warm days.
- Moderate nutrient demand; even moisture. Often direct sown in successions.
- Growth duration: about 6-8 weeks to a leaf harvest in the main season,
  longer for late autumn sowings that overwinter.

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow in rows; thin lightly. Pre-cultivation is unusual.
- Sowing: about February to April for late spring harvest, and about August to
  October for autumn, winter, and early spring harvest.
- Sowing depth: 2-3 cm.

- Spacing: about 20-25 cm between rows, a few cm within the row.
- Site: sun to light shade; cool conditions; humus-rich, moisture-retentive
  soil. Warm long days trigger bolting.

## Harvest & Use
- Cut whole young plants or pick outer leaves. Overwintered stands are cut in
  early spring.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: 50 days as an inferred planning value for a main-season leaf
  harvest from direct sowing. Garden sources give about 10-12 weeks to a full
  plant but note a usable leaf harvest after roughly 6-8 weeks
  ([meine-ernte](https://www.meine-ernte.de/pflanzen-a-z/gemuese/spinat/),
  [Plantura](https://www.plantura.garden/gemuese/spinat/spinat-anbauen)).
  50 days sits at the leaf-harvest end and is documented as inferred. The live
  variety record `Matador` stores 49 days. Overwintering sowings take much
  longer in calendar terms.
- Harvest window: 21 days as a planning value; a spinach stand is usable for
  only a short period before bolting or quality loss. The live variety record
  `Matador` also stores 21 days.
- Yield: about 2.5 kg/m² is the stored general value; not re-derived here.
- Thousand kernel weight: 12 g as a planning value. Spinach seed is commonly
  about 50-80 seeds per gram, i.e. roughly 12-20 g per thousand
  ([Osborne Seed - Seed Count Chart](https://www.osborneseed.com/pages/seed-count-chart)).
  12 g is at the lower, small-seeded end; round-seeded types can be heavier.
- Propagation duration: left empty. Spinach is direct sown.
