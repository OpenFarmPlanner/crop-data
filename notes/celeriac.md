## Short Description
- Celeriac (`Knollensellerie`) is `Apium graveolens var. rapaceum`, grown for the
  swollen hypocotyl/root "tuber". It is the same species as stalk celery
  (`Staudensellerie`, `Apium graveolens var. dulce`, see `celery.md`) but is
  deliberately kept as a separate crop (Kultur): different harvest form
  (storage tuber vs. leaf stalks) and a much longer, storage-oriented crop
  calendar. Leaf celery and bundle celery are not covered here.
- Cool-temperate biennial grown as an annual; long culture, `high` nutrient
  demand, even moisture, frost-sensitive as a young plant. Boron deficiency
  causes heart rot. Earlier drafts of this note used the qualifier "moderate
  to high"; `high` is now used as the single stored value because celeriac's
  full-season nutrient draw and boron sensitivity are comparable to other
  `high`-demand crops in this repository (see `Planning Value Derivations`
  for the reasoning); no source gives a lower categorical classification.
- Growth duration: 135 days from planting (planning value; source range about
  120-150 days from planting; harvest window 28 days; propagation duration 63
  days). Range per
  [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Knollensellerie_und_Bundsellerie_Erwerbsanbau),
  roughly 6-7 months from sowing according to garden guidance.

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: pre-cultivation under glass; seed is a light germinator, only
  pressed on and kept moist, germination in about 6-15 days at 18-22 degrees C
  ([Sativa Rheinau](https://www.sativa-rheinau.ch/hausgarten/tipps/kulturblaetter/knollensellerie.html)).
  Plant out after frost risk; early planting under film is possible in mild
  regions from mid-March, otherwise April to May.
- Sowing: about January to March (under glass).
- Outdoor planting: about April to May; about 8-10 weeks after sowing (inferred
  from the sowing and planting windows, not a direct source figure).
- Sowing depth: about 0.5 cm; celeriac is a light germinator and seed is
  pressed on or barely covered (see `Planning Value Derivations`).

- Spacing: 40-50 cm between rows; garden sources use 40 x 40 cm, commercial
  sources about 5-6 plants/m².
- Site: full sun, deep humus-rich, evenly moist soil. Plant with the growing
  point at soil level (not deep). Removing some soil from the upper roots once
  the tuber is walnut sized is a common practice.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest from about September to October before hard frost; well-formed
  tubers store cool and moist (moist sand, frost-free) until about
  February/March.
- Use: fresh, cooked, soups; leaves as seasoning.

## Notes
- Rotation: 3-4 years before Apiaceae again.
- Thousand kernel weight: about 0.3-0.6 g (0.4-0.6 g Hortipendium, 0.3-0.5 g
  Sativa Rheinau).

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: 135 days, inferred midpoint of the 120-150 day
  from-planting range; add pre-cultivation separately (about 8-10 weeks).
  Uncertain by about +/- 15 days by variety, site and planting date. Counted
  from transplanting, not sowing.
- Yield: 4.6 kg/m² (`per_sqm`). Source conflict, resolved: this note
  previously used 3.5 kg/m² (inferred midpoint of a 30-40 t/ha marketable
  yield range from [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Knollensellerie_und_Bundsellerie_Erwerbsanbau)).
  Re-checked against the
  [Statistisches Bundesamt vegetable table](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Themen/Branchen-Unternehmen/Landwirtschaft-Forstwirtschaft-Fischerei/Obst-Gemuese-Gartenbau/Tabellen/betriebe-anbau-erntemenge-gemuese.html),
  which gives a 2025 national average of 460.6 dt/ha (4.606 kg/m², rounded to
  4.6). The official statistic is preferred as the single planning value
  because it is a measured national average rather than Hortipendium's
  narrower estimated range; the two are in the same order of magnitude and
  not a large contradiction.
- Nutrient demand: `high`. Hortipendium's commercial cultivation page
  discusses celeriac among the more heavily fertilized vegetable crops
  (detailed N/P/K schedule referenced but not fully extractable from the
  page) and repeatedly flags boron as a limiting nutrient requiring
  monitoring; combined with the crop's long, full-season growth and the
  destatis-confirmed high marketable yield, `high` is used instead of the
  earlier "moderate to high" wording. No source suggests a `medium` or `low`
  classification.
- Sowing depth: 0.5 cm (5 mm), taken directly from
  [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Knollensellerie_und_Bundsellerie_Erwerbsanbau),
  consistent with the light-germinator description already in this note.
- Harvest window: 28 days, inferred. No source gives a per-planting window.
  Basis: planting April to May plus 135 days gives first harvest from about
  mid-August (early plantings) to mid-October (late plantings), while sources
  put the harvest between September and November before hard frost
  ([Sativa Rheinau](https://www.sativa-rheinau.ch/hausgarten/tipps/kulturblaetter/knollensellerie.html)
  lists August to October). Tubers can stay in the ground until frost, so
  about 4 weeks per planting is used. Not a source claim; uncertainty by
  weeks, and shorter for very late plantings.
- Propagation duration: 63 days, midpoint (9 weeks) of the 8-10 weeks nursery
  time in Hortipendium, cross-checked with the January-March sowing and
  April-May planting windows. Counted separately from the 135-day growth
  duration, which starts at planting.
- Thousand kernel weight: 0.4 g as a rounded planning value inside the
  0.3-0.6 g range; sources differ (see Research Status).
- Spacing: 0.40 x 0.40 m for planning (6.25 plants/m²), slightly above the
  commercial 5-6 plants/m².
