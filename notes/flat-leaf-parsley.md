## Short Description
- Glatte Petersilie (Kultur name; English name Flat-leaf parsley, English
  synonym Italian parsley; German synonyms `Blattpetersilie (glatt)` and
  `Italienische Petersilie`) is the smooth-leaved leaf type of
  `Petroselinum crispum`, usually written as `Petroselinum crispum var.
  neapolitanum` (see "Identity And Mapping Note" for the rank question). It is
  grown for repeated cuts of leaves and stalks, with a stronger aroma than the
  curly type according to garden sources.
- This note covers the smooth leaf type only. The curly type (Krause
  Petersilie) and the root type (Wurzelpetersilie) are separate notes.
- Apiaceae (Doldenblütler). Above-average potassium demand, sensitive to high
  salt levels at sowing, needs a wide crop rotation (more than 3 years) because
  of soil-borne diseases. Sun to partial shade, evenly moist, well prepared
  and firmly settled seedbed.
- Growth duration: 85 days from direct sowing to the first cut (planning
  value; 80-92 days depending on sowing date). Harvest window: 70 days (first
  cut plus two regrowth cuts). Both are planning values; see Planning Value
  Derivations.
- Expected yield: 6 kg/m² per season with three cuts (inferred planning
  value from a fertilization-yield level; see Planning Value Derivations).

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: for the earliest cut, 5-7 seeds per pressed-soil pot (3-4 cm),
  raised at 15/10 °C after up to 7 days of germination at about 18 °C, planted
  ball-deep at about 35-40 pots/m². Mostly a commercial early or protected
  method; the direct-sowing variant is the standard one and is modelled first.
- Sowing: 2026-01-15 to 2026-07-31 (pre-cultivation possible from mid-January;
  Rheinland-Pfalz table lists a mid-January sowing planted mid-March).
- Outdoor planting: 6-7 weeks after sowing (planning value 45 days).

### Direct Sowing
- Description: sow into a firm, well settled seedbed; keep evenly moist until
  emergence, which is slow. Do not sow deeper than 2 cm. Salt sensitivity: avoid
  heavy fertilizer placement at sowing.
- Sowing: 2026-03-01 to 2026-07-31 (early March, under fleece, to end of July;
  later sowings feed the overwintering crop below).
- Sowing depth: 1.5 cm (range 1-2 cm).

### Overwintering Crop
- Description: late-summer sowings can be overwintered outdoors or under film,
  with fleece as protection for very young stands. Suitability is variety
  dependent: the Rheinland-Pfalz variety table marks the Gigante types as not
  suitable for overwintering because they bolt, while smooth "Einfache" types
  such as Laura are listed as suitable.
- Sowing: 2026-08-18 to 2026-09-15.
- Harvest: spring of the following year, from about mid-April to early May,
  until bolting starts.

- Spacing: 25 cm between rows; 3 cm within the row (direct sowing, about 133
  plants/m²; garden guides use 30 x 20 cm)
- Site: sun to partial shade; humus-rich, evenly moist soil; avoid overhead
  irrigation late in the day because of downy mildew.

## Harvest & Use
- Cut the leaves and stalks by hand or machine; the first cut comes about
  80-92 days after direct sowing, follow-up cuts about 5 weeks later for the
  smooth type (about 6 weeks for the curly type). Up to 4 cuts per year are
  possible depending on the start of the crop.
- Cut in good time; late cutting favors downy mildew.
- Use: fresh herb in cooking, garnish, sauces and pesto; fresh storage at 0 °C
  and very high humidity for about 4 weeks (commercial).

## Notes
- Frost: leaves die back at about -7 to -10 °C, roots survive harder frost
  and regrow in spring; closed snow cover protects clearly. Overwintered plants
  in the second year bolt from about June and should not be harvested after
  bolting.
- Diseases: downy mildew is the main leaf disease; also Septoria (use healthy
  seed), Pythium in pots, and reddening or yellowing in summer (nematodes,
  carrot fly larvae, root aphids). Keep a rotation of more than 3 years.
- Nitrogen: for direct sowing apply nitrogen only from about the fourth week
  because of slow early growth; feed after each cut for quick regrowth.
- Seeds germinate slowly and in the dark; keep the bed moist.

## Differences To Krause Petersilie
- Cut interval: follow-up cuts after about 5 weeks (smooth) versus about 6
  weeks (curly); curly types add about 7 days to the crop duration of the
  reference crop
  ([DLR Rheinpfalz variety and cultivation sheet](https://www.bbs-landwirtschaft.de/Internet/global/themen.nsf/0/1ef319fcf05534dbc125876e002d5a6f/$FILE/Petersilie,%20Blatt.pdf)).
- Bolting: the same source rates the smooth Gigante types as bolting early
  (rating 4-5 on a scale where 9 means low bolting tendency) while most curly
  varieties score 6-8; several curly varieties are marked as suited for
  overwintering, and "Robust" as very frost tolerant.
- Aroma: [Plantura](https://www.plantura.garden/kraeuter/petersilie/petersilien-arten-und-sorten)
  describes smooth parsley as clearly more aromatic; the same page states that
  cultivation and care do not differ between the two leaf types.
- Not separated by sources: spacing, sowing depth, thousand kernel weight,
  germination time, nutrient demand and yield level are given for "leaf
  parsley" in general by [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Petersilie_Erwerbsanbau)
  and the DLR table; no flat-leaf-specific difference was found.
- Frost: a search-result summary suggested curly parsley is somewhat more
  cold tolerant; the source pages read did not confirm a measured difference.
- Independent corroboration (2026-09-29): a [GRAB 2019 organic variety
  trial](https://www.grab.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/5-compte-rendu-vari%C3%A9t%C3%A9s-persil-plat-et-fris%C3%A9-abris-GRAB-2019.pdf)
  grew flat-leaf and curly parsley side by side under identical conditions and
  spacing (15 plants/m², 25x27 cm), removing the "not separated by sources"
  gap for growth speed/yield specifically. At the same calendar date,
  flat-leaf's first cut averaged 1.50 kg/m² against 1.10 kg/m² for curly, and
  flat-leaf needed only 69 days between the first and second cut versus 82
  days for curly. This is real field-trial evidence, not a planning table,
  and independently confirms flat-leaf's faster growth and regrowth - unlike
  the analogous endive leaf-type split, which did not hold up under
  equivalent scrutiny (see `docs/endive-merge-2026-09-28.md`). Spacing itself
  was identical in the trial for both types, so that part of "not separated
  by sources" still stands.

## Identity And Mapping Note
- The former Kultur "Petersilie, Blatt" (`Petroselinum crispum`) is split into
  Glatte Petersilie (this note), Krause Petersilie and Wurzelpetersilie, because
  the lowest-cultivation-level rule applies and the leaf types differ in cut
  interval, bolting and overwintering suitability. Sources that only say
  "Petersilie" or "leaf parsley" without naming the leaf type are marked as
  unspecific.
- Botanical rank: the [DLR Rheinpfalz sheet](https://www.bbs-landwirtschaft.de/Internet/global/themen.nsf/0/1ef319fcf05534dbc125876e002d5a6f/$FILE/Petersilie,%20Blatt.pdf)
  and [ReinSaat](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/DE/gemuese/petersilie/)-style
  commercial sources use `var. neapolitanum` (flat-leaf) and `var. crispum`
  (curly). A search summary of [Plants of the World Online](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:60453819-2)
  states that `var. neapolitanum Danert` is treated as a synonym of
  `Petroselinum crispum subsp. crispum`; the POWO page itself returned an
  access error, so this is unverified. Some horticultural literature uses
  `convar.` for the leaf types; this was not verified. The common horticultural
  name `var. neapolitanum` is kept and the rank is left as an open question.
- Naming: "Italian parsley" is a common English synonym; bare "Parsley" or
  "Petersilie" is deliberately not used as a synonym (see the migration note).
- Bio Austria, LfL Bayern, Agroscope and KTBL pages could not be read in this
  session (see Research Status).

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: 85 days from direct sowing to first cut. Source basis: the
  DLR Rheinpfalz table lists 92 days (March), 91 (April), 80 (May) and 92 days
  (July) for field or film sowings, referring to a curly reference variety
  (curly types +7 days); [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Petersilie_Erwerbsanbau)
  gives 90 days (March/April) and 80 days (May to July);
  [Hortipendium - Hausgarten](https://www.hortipendium.de/Petersilie_im_Hausgarten)
  gives 80-95 days; ReinSaat's flat-leaf variety page points to a July sowing
  giving a good autumn yield. Derivation: rounded midpoint of the 80-92 day
  range; smooth types are not expected to be slower. Uncertainty: spring
  sowings under cool conditions take longer, summer sowings are quicker.
- Propagation duration: 45 days. Source basis: pre-cultivation of 35-60 days
  in 3-4 cm pots (Hortipendium), 40-59 days in the DLR table (47 days for
  Jan-March pots). Derivation: about 45 days from the middle of the ranges.
  Uncertainty: cool winter pre-cultivation takes longer; commercial method.
- Harvest window: 70 days. Source basis: DLR table (up to 4 cuts per year,
  follow-up cuts after about 5 weeks for smooth parsley; the table shows a
  second harvest for every listed sowing). Derivation: first cut plus two
  regrowth cuts at 35-day intervals = 70 days, an inferred planning value, not
  a source value. Uncertainty: a fourth cut in a long season would extend it to
  105 days; outdoor plantings from July sowings usually give only two cuts (35
  days).
- Sowing window (direct): 2026-03-01 to 2026-07-31. Source basis: Hortipendium
  lists direct sowing from 1 March to 1 September in the crop table, DLR from
  1 March (film) to 1 July for the first-year crop, ReinSaat's flat-leaf
  variety page early March to end of July. Derivation: the ReinSaat/DLR
  overlap was used. The overwintering window (2026-08-18 to 2026-09-15) comes
  from the DLR table. Year is a placeholder for a typical season.
- Sowing depth: 1.5 cm. Source basis: 1-2 cm (Hortipendium, ReinSaat), 1.5 cm
  ([Kiepenkerl](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/petersilie/)),
  1.2 cm (Hausgarten), maximum 2 cm (DLR). Derivation: midpoint, whole
  centimetre rule not required (fits the strictest known 0.5 cm steps).
- Spacing: 25 cm between rows, 3 cm within the row. Source basis: DLR and
  Hortipendium give 25-30 cm rows and 125-150 plants/m² for fresh-market direct
  sowing (20-40 cm rows in general); ReinSaat gives 30 x 20 cm for Gigante
  d'Italia (garden, thinned). Derivation: 133 plants/m² at 25 cm rows means 3.0
  cm between plants (100 cm / 25 cm x 3 cm spacing = 33 plants/m of row).
  Uncertainty: commercial density; garden spacing is much wider, and yield per
  plant is then higher.
- Yield: 6 kg/m² per season (three cuts). Source basis: the DLR table (data
  from LfL Freising, leaf parsley, type not stated) gives 300 dt/ha up to the
  first cut and 160 dt/ha after each further cut (240/160 with the industry
  dataset). Derivation: 300 dt/ha = 3.0 kg/m² for the first cut; 160 dt/ha =
  1.6 kg/m² per regrowth cut; 3.0 + 1.6 + 1.6 = 6.2, rounded to 6 kg/m² for a
  crop with three cuts (matching the harvest window). Uncertainty: this is a
  fertilization yield level for leaf parsley in general, not a measured
  marketable yield of smooth types, and the number of cuts strongly changes the
  season total (one cut 3, two cuts 4.6, four cuts 7.8 kg/m²).
- Thousand kernel weight: 1.5 g. Source basis: 1.2-1.8 g in Hortipendium and
  the DLR table (leaf parsley in general; 600-900 seeds per g). Derivation:
  midpoint.
- Nutrient demand: high (planning). Source basis: DLR table gives a nitrogen
  requirement value of 160 kg N/ha up to the first cut and 285 kg N/ha for all
  cuts (leaf parsley in general) and Hortipendium notes an above-average
  potassium need. Derivation: absolute nitrogen and potassium values are high
  for a vegetable crop. Uncertainty: no garden or Bio Austria classification of
  "Mittelzehrer" versus "Starkzehrer" was read; some garden lists may rate it
  medium.
- Germination time: 12-25 days. Source basis: 12 days at 20 °C, 25 days at
  12 °C, 45 days at 5 °C (Hortipendium), 15-25 days (Kiepenkerl), about 12 days
  at 20 °C (Hausgarten). Derivation: 14 days for warm outdoor soil (planning
  value).
- Frost tolerance and overwintering: qualitative, see Notes; source basis
  Hortipendium (foliage dies back at -7 to -10 °C, roots survive) and the DLR
  table (overwintering sowing window 18 August to 15 September).
- Crop family: Apiaceae (all sources).
