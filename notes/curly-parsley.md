## Short Description
- Krause Petersilie (Kultur name; English name Curly parsley) is the
  curly-leaved type of leaf parsley, `Petroselinum crispum var. crispum` (see
  "Identity And Mapping Note" for the botanical rank question). Leaves are
  dark green and ruffled ("Mooskrause" moss-curled types, or the "French
  type" with coarser curling). Used fresh as a herb and as a garnish.
- Apiaceae (Doldenblütler). Biennial, grown as an annual leaf crop with
  repeated cuts. Mostly direct sown; pre-cultivation in pots is used for the
  earliest harvest. Dark germinator, slow and irregular germination.
- Soil and site: loose, humus-rich, evenly moist soil; sun to partial shade.
  Keep a wide crop rotation (more than 3 years) against soil-borne diseases.
- Growth duration: 90 days from direct sowing to first cut (planning value);
  harvest window: 84 days for repeated cuts. See Planning Value Derivations.
- Expected yield: 5.6 kg/m² per crop cycle (about three cuts; planning value).

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: sow 5-7 seeds per pot or pressed-soil block, germinate warm
  (about 18 °C for up to 7 days in a germination room), then grow on cool.
  Plant the whole root ball. This variant serves the earliest harvest only.
- Sowing: 2026-02-01 to 2026-03-15
- Outdoor planting: 7 weeks after sowing.

### Direct Sowing
- Description: main method. Sow into a firm, well settled seedbed, shallow, and
  keep the surface moist until emergence (15-30 days).
- Sowing: 2026-04-01 to 2026-07-15
- Sowing depth: 1.5 cm (1-2 cm)

### Overwintering Crop
- Description: sow in late summer and harvest in the following spring; very
  young stands can winterkill, fleece may be needed.
- Sowing: 2026-08-18 to 2026-09-15
- Harvest: from mid-April to May of the following year (first cut in the
  second year, before flowering).

- Spacing: 25 cm between rows; 3 cm within the row (about 125-150 plants/m²)
- Site: sun to partial shade; loose, humus-rich soil; even water supply.

## Harvest & Use
- Cut the leaves with a low cutting height (bunches or loose in crates) once
  the stand has enough leaf mass; leave the growing point so it regrows. Up to
  four cuts per year, a follow-up cut about 6 weeks after the previous one.
- Stop harvesting when the second-year plants start to flower (about
  June-July).
- Use: fresh as a herb and garnish; also drying and freezing.

## Notes
- Frost: sources disagree on the exact limit (see Research Status); foliage is
  damaged below about -7 °C, roots survive lower temperatures under cover.
- Red and yellow discoloration in summer is a frequent problem (nematodes,
  carrot fly maggots, root aphids, hidden-feeding aphids). Downy mildew is the
  main leaf disease: wide rows, irrigate only early in the morning, cut in time.
- Avoid nitrogen excess; apply nitrogen in at least two portions and after each
  cut. Germination is inhibited by high salt levels near the seed.
- Storage after harvest: about 4 weeks at 0 °C and very high humidity.

## Differences To Glatte Petersilie
- Cutting interval: the Rhineland-Palatinate guidance gives about 6 weeks
  between cuts for curly and about 5 weeks for flat-leaf parsley, and adds
  about 7 days to the crop duration for curly or compact types
  ([DLR/RLP - Anbau- und Sortenhinweise Petersilie, Blatt](https://www.bbs-landwirtschaft.de/Internet/global/themen.nsf/0/1ef319fcf05534dbc125876e002d5a6f/$FILE/Petersilie,%20Blatt.pdf)).
- Bolting: the same variety tables rate curly types as less bolting-prone than
  most flat-leaf types (score 8 vs 4-5 for several "Gigante" types).
- Frost, leaf mass, aroma, habit: [Samen.de](https://samen.de/blog/krause-vs.-glatte-petersilie-untersuchung-der-unterschiede.html)
  says curly parsley tolerates light frost better, grows more slowly with less
  leaf mass, is compact (20-30 cm) and more robust in aroma, whereas flat-leaf
  parsley is taller (30-40 cm) and milder. This is a garden-level source only.
- Spacing, sowing depth, yield level, nutrient demand, thousand kernel weight:
  no source separates the two types; the sources speak about "Petersilie" or
  "Blattpetersilie" in general. The values below are therefore shared
  leaf-parsley values, not curly-specific measurements.
- Independent corroboration (2026-09-29): a [GRAB 2019 organic variety
  trial](https://www.grab.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/5-compte-rendu-vari%C3%A9t%C3%A9s-persil-plat-et-fris%C3%A9-abris-GRAB-2019.pdf)
  grew flat-leaf and curly parsley side by side under identical conditions and
  spacing (15 plants/m², 25x27 cm). At the same calendar date, curly's first
  cut averaged 1.10 kg/m² against 1.50 kg/m² for flat-leaf ("le rendement est
  beaucoup plus faible qu'en persil plat"); curly needed 82 days between the
  first and second cut versus 69 days for flat-leaf, a measured ~13-day gap
  matching the DLR/RLP "+7 days for curly" figure in direction; and curly
  plants were shorter at every cut (21 cm vs 29 cm at the first cut). This is
  real field-trial evidence, not a planning/fertilization table, and it
  independently confirms that curly parsley grows and regrows more slowly
  than flat-leaf under the same conditions - unlike the endive leaf-type
  split, which did not hold up under equivalent scrutiny (see
  `docs/endive-merge-2026-09-28.md`).

## Identity And Mapping Note
- The former Kultur "Petersilie, Blatt" (`Petroselinum crispum`) is split into
  three Kulturen: Krause Petersilie (this note), Glatte Petersilie and
  Wurzelpetersilie. Reason: lowest-cultivation-level rule; the types differ in
  cutting interval, frost tolerance and habit. No German synonym is prescribed
  here; bare "Petersilie" or "Parsley" is not used as a name (see the separate
  migration note).
- Botanical rank conflict: the [Rhineland-Palatinate guidance](https://www.bbs-landwirtschaft.de/Internet/global/themen.nsf/0/1ef319fcf05534dbc125876e002d5a6f/$FILE/Petersilie,%20Blatt.pdf)
  uses `P. c. var. crispum` for curly and `var. neapolitanum` for flat-leaf
  parsley. A search summary of [Plants of the World Online](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:77230977-1)
  lists `Petroselinum crispum subsp. crispum` and describes `var.
  neapolitanum` as not validly published (no type cited); the POWO page itself
  returned an error, so the accepted rank of `var. crispum` is unverified. The
  horticultural name `var. crispum` is kept; a cultivar-group treatment is
  possible.
- Broad category: shop headings such as "Kräuter" or "Petersilie" were not used
  as crop identity.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: 90 days from direct sowing to first cut. Source basis: the
  Rhineland-Palatinate table gives 80 days for a 1 May field sowing (first cut
  20 July, reference type flat-leaf) and 91-92 days for April and July
  sowings, plus about 7 days for curly types; [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Petersilie_Erwerbsanbau)
  gives 90-100 days for spring sowings. Derivation: 80 + 7 = 87, rounded to
  90 to match Hortipendium. Uncertainty: March sowings under film and
  autumn sowings differ strongly (up to 258 days for August sowings that are
  cut in spring).
- Propagation duration: 50 days. Source basis: the same table gives 47 days
  from pot sowing to planting for a mid-March planting and 59 days for a
  January sowing. Derivation: about 7 weeks, rounded to 50 days. Only for
  the pre-cultivated variant; direct sowing is the standard.
- Harvest window: 84 days. Source basis: up to four cuts per year, about 6
  weeks between cuts for curly parsley. Derivation: first cut plus two
  follow-up cuts at 42 days = 84 days. Inferred planning value, not a direct
  source value; the season can be longer with a fourth cut or overwintering.
- Sowing windows: direct sowing 2026-04-01 to 2026-07-15. Source basis:
  [Kiepenkerl](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/petersilie/) says
  as soon as the soil is frost free until mid-June; [ReinSaat](https://www.biogartenbedarf.de/bio-saatgut/kr%C3%A4uter-saatgut/petersilie-gr%C3%BCne-perle/)
  (variety Grüne Perle, via a retailer listing) gives February under glass and
  April to mid-July in the field; the Rhineland-Palatinate table lists
  field sowings from 1 May to 15 September. Derivation: April to mid-July.
  The year is a placeholder for a typical season. Overwintering sowings follow
  the Rhineland-Palatinate window 18 August to 15 September.
- Sowing depth: 1.5 cm. Kiepenkerl and ReinSaat give 1.5 cm, Hortipendium 1-2
  cm, the Rhineland-Palatinate guidance at most 2 cm.
- Spacing: 25 cm between rows, 3 cm within the row. Source basis: row spacing
  25-30 cm and 125-150 plants/m² for fresh market
  (Rhineland-Palatinate); ReinSaat 25-30 cm; Kiepenkerl and Hortipendium 20
  cm. Derivation: 25 cm row spacing at 133 plants/m² gives 100 / 25 = 4 m of
  row per m², so 133 / 4 = 33 plants per metre = 3 cm. Uncertainty: single
  seeds per position are not realistic; the in-row value is an average.
- Yield: 5.6 kg/m² per crop cycle. Source basis: the Rhineland-Palatinate
  fertilization data give a yield (field removal) of 240 dt/ha up to the first
  cut and 160 dt/ha after it (about 300 and 160 dt/ha of growth). Derivation:
  240 dt/ha = 2.4 kg/m², 160 dt/ha = 1.6 kg/m² per later cut; three cuts =
  2.4 + 1.6 + 1.6 = 5.6 kg/m². Uncertainty: level for fertilization planning,
  not a measured marketable yield; unspecific to curly parsley (see Differences).
- Thousand kernel weight: 1.5 g. Source basis: 1.2-1.8 g
  (Rhineland-Palatinate); 1.32 g for the curly variety Grüne Perle (ReinSaat
  via retailer). Derivation: midpoint of the general range.
- Germination time: 21 days. Source basis: 15-30 days at 20 °C (ReinSaat via
  retailer), 15-25 days (Kiepenkerl). Derivation: about the middle of the
  range.
- Nutrient demand: medium. Source basis: the Rhineland-Palatinate data give a
  nitrogen requirement value of 160 kg N/ha up to the first cut;
  [Kiepenkerl](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/petersilie/)
  implies a medium feeder; Hortipendium notes an above-average potassium
  demand (K2O about 159 kg/ha to the first cut). Derivation: medium chosen;
  arguably medium to high.
- Crop family: Apiaceae (general botanical knowledge; all sources agree on
  the genus).
- Frost tolerance and overwintering: qualitative, no planning field.

## Research Status
- Researched on: 2026-09-25; leaf-type split re-checked against independent
  field-trial evidence on 2026-09-29 (see Differences To Glatte Petersilie)
- General crop note exists: not applicable (this is the general crop note)
- Open source conflicts:
  - Frost limit: about -7 °C foliage dieback (Hortipendium) versus survival
    to about -10 °C (search summary of a seed-company text); not settled.
  - Row spacing: 20 cm (Kiepenkerl, Hortipendium) versus 25-30 cm
    (Rhineland-Palatinate, ReinSaat).
  - Time to first cut: 80-92 days (table, flat-leaf reference) versus 90-100
    days (Hortipendium).
  - Curly frost hardiness versus flat-leaf rests on one garden source.
  - Growth speed/yield gap between leaf types: no longer only a single
    planning-table claim; the 2026-09-29 GRAB field trial independently
    confirms curly parsley grows and regrows more slowly than flat-leaf under
    identical conditions (see Differences To Glatte Petersilie). This is
    treated as resolved in favor of keeping the leaf-type split, unlike the
    analogous endive question.
- Open mapping questions: split of "Petersilie, Blatt" into three Kulturen;
  botanical rank of `var. crispum` (POWO page not opened; cultivar-group
  treatment possible); yield unit per cut versus per season (per season used);
  harvest window is inferred; overwintering and transplant variants are only
  partly modelled. LfL Bayern basic-data tables and Bio Austria were not
  read in detail (LfL page had no parsley data; Bio Austria yielded nothing
  specific), and ReinSaat's own page was not reachable (variety data via a
  retailer listing). Sources rarely separate curly from flat-leaf parsley.
- Public-readiness: needs review (inferred harvest window, yield not
  type-specific, botanical rank unverified).
