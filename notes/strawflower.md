## Short Description
- Strawflower (`Strohblume`) is `Xerochrysum bracteatum` (syn. `Helichrysum
  bracteatum`), a warm-season annual grown as a cut and dried flower.
- Frost-sensitive; full sun; low to moderate nutrient demand; tolerates dry
  conditions once established.
- Growth duration: about 8-9 weeks from sowing to first flowers, then a long
  cutting period.

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: pre-cultivate for an early start; the seed is a light
  germinator, so cover only lightly.
- Sowing: about March to April under protection.
- Outdoor planting: after the last frost.

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow after the last frost.
- Sowing depth: barely covered (light germinator).

- Spacing: about 25-30 cm each way.
- Site: full sun, well-drained soil; wind-sheltered so stems stay straight.

## Harvest & Use
- Cut flowers as the outer bracts open but the centre is still closed, for
  fresh or dried use; frequent cutting extends flowering over many weeks.

## Comparison With General Crop Data

## Planning Value Derivations
- Thousand kernel weight: 0.6 g as a planning value, reused from the project's
  `niedrig` variety record (0.64 g). No separate general seed-weight source was
  needed; strawflower seed is consistently very light.
- Yield: intentionally left open. Strawflower is a cut-flower crop; a per-m²
  weight yield is not a meaningful planning value and no reliable source frames
  it that way.
