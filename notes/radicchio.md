## Short Description
- Radicchio is the red heading leaf chicory `Cichorium intybus var. foliosum`,
  grown for firm red heads for autumn and winter harvest. Colour and heart
  firm up with cool nights.
- Cool-season, frost-tolerant to a degree. Moderate nutrient demand, even
  moisture.
- Growth duration: about 60-100 days depending on type, roughly 10-12 weeks
  from germination for autumn types.

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow into the final position, thin to stand; also grown
  from transplants.
- Sowing: about June to August for autumn and winter harvest; early types from
  mid June, winter types to early August.
- Sowing depth: 1-2 cm.

- Spacing: about 30 cm between rows, 25 cm within the row.
- Site: full sun, deep moisture-retentive soil. Winter types with protection
  can stand into February.

## Harvest & Use
- Cut heads once the heart has fully coloured and feels firm, from autumn
  onward. Winter types are harvested gradually through the cold season.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: 85 days as an inferred planning value for an autumn/winter
  radicchio. Garden sources give about 10-12 weeks from germination and a
  60-100 day span by type
  ([meine-ernte](https://www.meine-ernte.de/pflanzen-a-z/gemuese/radicchio/),
  [Sperli](https://www.sperli.de/anbauexperte/kulturanleitung-radicchio/)).
  85 days is a whole-day mid value for autumn/winter types; documented as
  inferred. The live variety record `Rossa di Chioggia/Vesuvio`, a faster type,
  stores 55 days.
- Harvest window: 30 days as a planning value, matching the live variety
  record; heads are cut over a fairly short autumn window, longer for protected
  winter types.
- Yield: left open as a general value. The live variety record stores about
  3.0 kg/m²; a general value should be confirmed against head weight and
  spacing before being stored.
- Harvest method: left open until a general yield basis exists.
- Thousand kernel weight: 1.4 g as a planning value. Chicory seed is about
  600-900 seeds per gram, i.e. roughly 1.1-1.7 g per thousand
  ([Osborne Seed - Seed Count Chart](https://www.osborneseed.com/pages/seed-count-chart)).
