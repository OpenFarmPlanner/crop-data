## Short Description
- Rutabaga/swede (`Steckrübe`, `Kohlrübe`) is `Brassica napus` subsp.
  `rapifera` (also written `var. napobrassica`), grown for its large,
  storable root; leaves are edible but secondary.
- Different species from turnip (`Mairübe`/`Herbstrübe`, `Brassica rapa`);
  German-language shop categories sometimes group both under a general
  "Rüben"/"Turnips" label, so identity must be checked per variety rather than
  assumed from a shop category (see `notes/rutabaga-helenor.md` for a worked
  example).
- Biennial grown as an annual; long-season, very storage-capable winter root.
  Growth duration: about 90-100 days from sowing to harvest is a commonly
  cited general planning value (see `Planning Value Derivations`).

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow, or pre-cultivate on a seedbed and transplant after
  the fourth or fifth true leaf; a long-season crop generally sown once in
  early summer for an autumn/winter harvest.
- Sowing: from about May.
- Sowing depth: 1-2 cm.

- Spacing: about 40-50 cm between rows, 30-40 cm within the row.
- Site: sun; humus-rich, loamy soil that holds moisture well. Net cover
  against cabbage fly and flea beetles is useful while plants are young.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest in autumn after one or more frosts, which is commonly said to
  improve flavor; roots store well over winter in a cool, humid place.
- Eaten cooked (mashed, roasted, in stews); a traditional but currently
  under-used winter vegetable.

## Notes
- Do not confuse with turnip (`Mairübe`/`Herbstrübe`, `Brassica rapa`), a
  faster-growing, smaller, and generally milder root vegetable. Some seed-shop
  category pages (for example a general "Turnips" category) list both crops
  together; the crop identity of each individual variety must be verified
  against breeder/seed-catalog sources rather than inferred from the shop
  category label alone. This note only covers `Brassica napus`
  Steckrübe/rutabaga.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Definitions: rutabaga is direct sown, so growth duration is counted from
  sowing to first harvest; propagation duration is left empty (optional
  seedbed pre-cultivation is not the main method).
- Growth duration: 95 days used as an inferred midpoint of the commonly cited
  "about 90-100 days" rutabaga planning range seen across seed-catalog sources
  for this crop type (see `notes/rutabaga-helenor.md` for a variety-specific
  100-day source value). Documented as inferred rather than a single direct
  source figure for the general crop.
- Harvest window: 60 days, inferred. Roots are harvestable from late September
  and are commonly lifted in October and November (regionally mid-September to
  end of November) and should be out by about Christmas because they turn
  woody ([Gartenjournal](https://www.gartenjournal.net/steckrueben-anbau),
  [samen.de](https://samen.de/blog/kohlrueben-ernten-richtiger-zeitpunkt-und-beste-qualitaet.html)).
  60 days (about October to November) is a representative window for one
  planting; storage practice allows a single lift.
- The same sources give a vegetation period of 90-120 days from sowing, which
  is consistent with the 95-day planning value at its lower end.
- Field/form constraint: whole days.
- Yield: no reliable general commercial-yield figure in kg/m² was found. A
  large-scale field figure of roughly 500 dt/ha (about 5 kg/m²) appears in one
  source discussing game-feed cultivation
  ([nutzpflanzenvielfalt.de](https://www.nutzpflanzenvielfalt.de/steckr%C3%BCbe)-style
  guidance), but this reflects a different cultivation purpose (deer forage
  plots) rather than garden/market cultivation, so it is not adopted as the
  general planning value; left open instead.
