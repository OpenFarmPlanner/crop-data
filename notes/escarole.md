## Short Description
- Glatte Endivie (Kultur name; English name Escarole; synonyms `Escariol`,
  `Eskariol`, `Endiviensalat`) is the
  smooth, broad-leaved type of `Cichorium endivia`, usually written as
  `Cichorium endivia var. latifolium` (see "Identity And Mapping Note" for the
  naming question). It forms a fairly open to compact head of broad, fleshy,
  slightly wavy leaves whose inner leaves turn pale yellow, either on their own
  (self-blanching types) or after blanching.
- This note covers escarole only. The curly type (Kultur `Krause Endivie`, trade name `Frisée`, var. crispum) is a
  separate crop note, because the two types differ in frost hardiness, bolting
  tolerance and best planting dates (see "Differences To Krause Endivie
  (Frisée)").
- Asteraceae (Korbblütler). Sown in early summer, transplanted in July to
  August and harvested in autumn; the type is regarded as very frost tolerant
  and is the preferred type for late autumn and early winter harvest. Medium
  nutrient demand, even moisture, sun to partial shade.
- Growth duration: 70 days from transplanting to harvest maturity (about 95
  days from sowing including 25 days of young-plant cultivation). Harvest
  window: 21 days. Both are planning values; see Planning Value Derivations.
- Expected yield: 6 kg/m² (planning value from a fertilization-regulation
  yield level; see Planning Value Derivations and the conflict noted in
  Research Status).

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: raise young plants in small pots or pressed-soil blocks, one
  seed per pot, warm (about 20 °C for germination, and keep young plants
  warm afterwards) to avoid a cold shock that can trigger bolting. Sow shallow,
  as endive germinates in light and warm soil above about 20 °C germinates
  poorly. Plant out at soil level.
- Sowing: 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-20 (early June to mid/late July for a summer
  planting and autumn to early winter harvest; winter-harvest sowings are
  mid to late July).
- Outdoor planting: 3-4 weeks after sowing (planting in July to August).

### Direct Sowing
- Description: possible from mid-June according to one garden source, but
  transplants are the standard method in commercial and organic practice, so
  this variant is not modelled here.

- Sowing depth: about 1 cm (0.5-1 cm in commercial sources; 2-3 cm in garden
  seed-company guides).
- Spacing: about 35 cm between rows; 30 cm within the row (sources give 30-40
  cm between rows and 30-35 cm within the row, about 8-11 plants/m²).
- Site: sun to partial shade; evenly moist, well supplied soil with balanced
  nutrition; avoid excess nitrogen, which promotes crown rot and calcium
  deficiency symptoms.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest whole heads once the head is full and the inner leaves are pale.
  Blanching (light-tight hood, plate or binding, roughly one week before
  harvest) reduces bitterness; self-blanching types need less.
- Sources describe harvest of the smooth type from about September, with
  heads able to stay in the field into November or December because of their
  frost tolerance.
- Use: raw in salads (mild taste), also lightly cooked.

## Notes
- Frost: the smooth type is described as very frost tolerant and harvestable
  into December; one garden guide names smooth types tolerating down to about
  -5 °C, another only about -1 °C (see Research Status).
- Bolting: cold spells below roughly 17 °C combined with long days induce
  bolting, which cannot be reversed once induced. This is the main reason for
  not planting escarole too early; early plantings before mid-June are
  generally given to curly types.
- Bolting risk and crown rot (Kranzfäule) are the main risks; keep a 3-4 year
  break for endive on the same bed.

## Differences To Krause Endivie (Frisée)
- Frost: garden and seed-company guides describe escarole as clearly more
  frost tolerant and better for late harvest than the curly type
  ([Sperli](https://www.sperli.de/anbauexperte/kulturanleitung-endivien/),
  [Kiepenkerl](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/endivien/)); one
  source claims the opposite (see Research Status).
- Bolting and early planting: curly types are recommended for plantings before
  mid-June because they are less sensitive to cold and bolt less
  ([Kiepenkerl](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/endivien/)).
- Nitrogen demand: the fertilization-regulation table lists a higher
  nitrogen requirement and a higher average yield level for smooth endive than
  for curly endive (190 vs 150 kg N/ha; 600 vs 350 dt/ha), see Planning Value
  Derivations
  ([LWK NRW](https://www.landwirtschaftskammer.de/gartenbau/beratung/pdf/n-bedarf-gemuese-nrw.pdf)).
- Blanching and spacing: no source-backed difference was found; a commercial
  source states that the blanching methods apply equally to both types
  ([Hortipendium - Endivien Erwerbsanbau](https://www.hortipendium.de/Endivien_Erwerbsanbau)).

## Identity And Mapping Note
- The former Kultur "Endiviensalat" (`Cichorium endivia`) is split by cultivation
  type. This note is the Kultur `Glatte Endivie` (escarole, smooth); the curly type is a
  separate note. Reason: the lowest-cultivation-level rule; the types differ in
  frost hardiness, bolting behaviour and planting window. Sources that only
  say "Endivie" without naming the type are marked as unspecific below and
  must not be read as escarole-specific.
- Botanical naming: [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Endivien_Erwerbsanbau)
  uses `Cichorium endivia var. latifolium` (smooth) and `var. crispum` (curly).
  A search summary of [Plants of the World Online](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:194527-1)
  indicated that `var. crispum` is treated as the accepted name of the
  species-level entry with `var. latifolium` as a separate variety, and
  [Pl@ntUse](https://plantuse.plantnet.org/en/Cichorium_endivia_Latifolium_Group_(Common_names))
  treats the smooth type as a cultivar group ("Latifolium Group"). The POWO
  page itself could not be opened (access blocked), so the accepted status of
  `var. latifolium` is unverified; the widely used horticultural name is kept.
- Naming: bare "Endive" is not used here because in US English it means
  chicory (`Cichorium intybus`, Belgian endive). The English name is
  "Escarole". The German Kultur name is "Glatte Endivie" (chosen 2026-09-24 as the
  self-explanatory name, symmetric to "Krause Endivie"); "Escariol"
  (spelled "Eskariol" by some sources) and "Endiviensalat" are kept as
  synonyms.
- Sources that call the crop "Endivien" in general (Bio Austria style
  fertilization tables, seed-company guides) were used only for values noted
  as unspecific.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: 70 days, counted from transplanting to harvest maturity.
  Source basis: the Austrian fertilization guideline lists an endive crop of
  10 weeks (type not stated)
  ([Richtlinien fuer die sachgerechte Duengung](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/Landesnormen/LST40018704/6930.00-34_Anl4.pdf));
  a garden guide gives 10-12 weeks after sowing for both types
  ([Samen.de](https://samen.de/blog/unterschiede-zwischen-eskariol-und-frisee-welche-endivienart-passt-zu-ihrem-garten.html)),
  and one seed package reference gives 16 weeks for a winter escarole (from a
  search summary, not verified). Derivation: 10 weeks = 70 days after planting
  from the Austrian table; with 25 days of young-plant cultivation this gives
  about 95 days from sowing, which fits the general remark of 90-100 days from
  sowing. Uncertainty: unspecific to the type; late plantings for winter
  harvest and cold autumn weather lengthen the crop clearly (12-16 weeks).
- Propagation duration: 25 days. Source basis: 3-4 weeks from sowing to
  planting out
  ([Sperli](https://www.sperli.de/anbauexperte/kulturanleitung-endivien/),
  [Kiepenkerl](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/endivien/)).
  Derivation: 21-28 days, rounded to 25 days.
- Harvest window: 21 days. Source basis: no source gives a harvest window of
  one planting. Escarole is harvestable from about September into November or
  December, and self-blanching or blanched heads hold for some time in the
  field. Derivation: inferred value of three weeks for one planting date
  (heads should be cut within about 2-3 weeks of maturity, more in cool
  weather). Uncertainty: this is an inferred planning value, not a source
  value; cool autumn weather allows a longer standing period.
- Sowing window: 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-20. Source basis:
  [ReinSaat](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/DE/salate/endiviensalat/) gives
  sowing from early June, winter-harvest sowings in mid to late July and
  planting in July to August; [Sperli](https://www.sperli.de/anbauexperte/kulturanleitung-endivien/)
  gives early May to early July; [Kiepenkerl](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/endivien/)
  early May to mid July; [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Endivien_Erwerbsanbau)
  lists succession plantings from early March to mid-August (both types
  together, not escarole-specific). Derivation: the ReinSaat window was used
  because the escarole type should not be planted early (bolting risk). Earlier
  sowings (May) are possible but the guides advise curly types for them. The
  year in the dates is a placeholder for a typical season.
- Sowing depth: 1 cm; [ReinSaat](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/DE/col/escariol_gruener/)
  gives 1 cm, [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Endivien_Erwerbsanbau)
  5-10 mm; Sperli and Kiepenkerl give 2-3 cm.
- Spacing: 35 cm between rows, 30 cm within the row, about 9.5 plants/m².
  Source basis: ReinSaat 30-40 x 30 cm; Kiepenkerl 30 x 35 cm; Hortipendium 38
  x 30 cm (8.9 plants/m²) and 33 x 33 cm (8.0 plants/m²); a south Baden
  planting-distance table gives 30-35 x 30-35 cm (8-11 plants/m²)
  (a south Baden variety and planting-distance sheet of a district
  agricultural office, 2021; the original URL was not recorded in this session,
  so it is not listed under Sources and is a supplement only). Derivation: mid-values of the ranges (35 x 30 cm). All spacing
  values are for endive in general or for escarole varieties of seed
  companies; no type-specific spacing difference was found.
- Yield: 6 kg/m². Source basis: the German fertilization regulation table as
  published by [LWK NRW](https://www.landwirtschaftskammer.de/gartenbau/beratung/pdf/n-bedarf-gemuese-nrw.pdf)
  gives an average yield level of 600 dt/ha for smooth-leaved endive (350
  dt/ha for curly). Derivation: 600 dt/ha = 6 kg/m². At about 9.5 plants/m²
  this equals about 0.63 kg per head. Uncertainty: this is a
  fertilization planning level, not a measured marketable yield; the Austrian
  guideline gives 30000-40000 kg/ha (3-4 kg/m²) for a 10-week endive crop
  without type. See Research Status.
- Thousand kernel weight: 1.5 g. Source basis: [ReinSaat](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/DE/col/escariol_gruener/)
  gives 1.47 g for the variety Escariol grüner; the ReinSaat category page gives
  1.3-2 g and [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Endivien_Erwerbsanbau)
  1.3-1.6 g (endive in general). Derivation: rounded to 1.5 g.
- Nutrient demand: medium. Source basis: [BUND Region Hannover](https://bund-region-hannover.de/fileadmin/hannover/BUND_aktiv/Universum_Kleingarten/Publikationen_zum_Download/Handreichung_Stark-_Mittel-_und_Schwachzehrer.pdf)
  lists endive as a medium feeder (type not stated). The nitrogen requirement
  values are 190 kg N/ha for smooth endive
  ([LWK NRW](https://www.landwirtschaftskammer.de/gartenbau/beratung/pdf/n-bedarf-gemuese-nrw.pdf))
  and 150 kg N/ha for a 10-week endive crop in the Austrian guideline; these
  are context values.
- Frost tolerance, blanching, bolting: qualitative, no planning field. See
  Notes and Harvest & Use.

## Research Status
- Researched on: 2026-09-24
- General crop note exists: not applicable (this is the general crop note)
- Open source conflicts:
  - Frost: [Samen.de](https://samen.de/blog/unterschiede-zwischen-eskariol-und-frisee-welche-endivienart-passt-zu-ihrem-garten.html)
    says curly types tolerate about -3 °C and escarole only about -1 °C;
    [Sperli](https://www.sperli.de/anbauexperte/kulturanleitung-endivien/) and
    [Kiepenkerl](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/endivien/) say
    the reverse (escarole very frost tolerant, smooth types to about -5 °C).
    No official source (LfL, Bio Austria, Agroscope) was found to settle it.
  - Yield: 6 kg/m² (fertilization level for smooth endive) versus 3-4 kg/m²
    (Austrian guideline, type not stated).
  - Growth duration: 10 weeks (Austrian guideline, presumably from
    planting) versus 10-12 weeks from sowing (garden guide) versus up to 16
    weeks (winter escarole).
  - Sowing depth: about 1 cm versus 2-3 cm.
  - The requested primary sources (LfL Bayern, Bio Austria) yielded no
    escarole-specific pages in this session; values rest on ReinSaat,
    Hortipendium, German/Austrian fertilization tables and seed-company guides.
- Open mapping questions: the split of the former endive Kultur into escarole
  and curly endive; botanical name `var. latifolium` vs cultivar-group
  treatment (POWO page not opened); English name "Escarole" replaces the
  ambiguous "Endive". Direct sowing and winter-harvest variants are not
  modelled. Harvest window is inferred.
- Public-readiness: needs review (harvest window inferred, growth duration and
  yield not type-verified, no LfL/Bio Austria source read).
