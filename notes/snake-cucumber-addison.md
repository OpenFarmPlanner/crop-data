## Short Description
- `Addison` is treated here as a `Schlangengurke` (`Cucumis sativus`), a
  long-fruited slicing cucumber. **This mapping is lower-confidence than
  `Tanja` and `Arola`**: unlike those two, `Addison` was not found in the
  ReinSaat catalog (neither on the
  [Schlangengurken category page](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/DE/gurken/schlangengurken/)
  nor elsewhere on the site). The only source found is the breeder
  [Rijk Zwaan - Addison F1](https://www.rijkzwaan.de/gurke/ADDISON-F1-24-151-prdCS10683-ctgCrops.cucumber),
  a professional commercial hybrid seed listing, not a home-garden or organic
  seed source. See `Research Status` for the explicit identity caveat.
- `Addison F1` is described as a long cucumber (`lange Gurke`) with an open,
  easy-to-work growth habit, strong resistance to tipburn (`Kopfbrenner`),
  generative growth pattern, early fruit set, and uniform fruits with less
  pronounced ribbing; suitable for ground (soil-grown) cultivation as well as
  the usual trellised greenhouse cultivation
  ([Rijk Zwaan - Addison F1](https://www.rijkzwaan.de/gurke/ADDISON-F1-24-151-prdCS10683-ctgCrops.cucumber)).
- Fruit size: about 30-33 cm long, approximately 390-430 g per fruit
  ([Rijk Zwaan - Addison F1](https://www.rijkzwaan.de/gurke/ADDISON-F1-24-151-prdCS10683-ctgCrops.cucumber)).
- As an F1 hybrid, `Addison` is not seed-stable/nachbaufähig, unlike the
  open-pollinated `Tanja`.
- Growth duration: not given as a day-count by the source; left open, matching
  the general `Schlangengurke` crop (see `Comparison With General Crop Data`).

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: no `Addison`-specific pre-cultivation instructions were found

- Sowing: not given by the source; see general crop note for typical timing.
- Outdoor planting: not given by the source; see general crop note (after the
  Ice Saints, mid-to-late May).

- Spacing: not given by the source in a directly usable cm figure. Left open
  rather than assumed equal to `Tanja`/`Arola`, since `Addison` is a distinct,
  larger-fruited commercial hybrid and spacing needs for such types can differ
  from open-pollinated garden varieties.
- Site: full sun, warm; the source notes the variety is bred for an open,
  easy-to-manage plant habit and suitability for soil-grown cultivation,
  suggesting flexibility between greenhouse and open-field/tunnel growing, but
  this is not stated as explicitly as for `Tanja`/`Arola`.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest once fruits reach the described size (30-33 cm, about 390-430 g);
  the source's emphasis on "early fruit set" suggests earlier first harvest
  than open-pollinated types, but no day-count is given.
- Used fresh, as a long slicing cucumber.

## Notes
- Tipburn resistance is the source's headline trait; tipburn is a
  physiological leaf-edge disorder linked to calcium/water management, mainly
  relevant for intensive commercial cultivation.
- Because `Addison` is an F1 hybrid from a professional breeder catalog rather
  than a home-garden/organic seed source, its cultivation guidance is thinner
  than for `Tanja`/`Arola`; several fields below are left open rather than
  inferred from other varieties.

## Comparison With General Crop Data
- Fruit length (30-33 cm) is broadly consistent with the general crop's
  "long, slender" slicing cucumber framing and close to `Tanja`'s 25-30 cm,
  but no direct general-crop value exists to compare against numerically.
- Spacing: left open. The general crop note's disputed range (40-130 cm
  depending on training method) is not silently applied to `Addison`, since
  the source gives no variety-specific figure and `Addison`'s larger,
  heavier fruit could plausibly need different spacing than `Tanja`/`Arola`.
  This is documented as an open question rather than a silent reuse.
- Growth duration and harvest window: no `Addison`-specific day-count found;
  left open, consistent with the general `Schlangengurke` note's open value.
- Yield: no kg/m² or piece-count figure found; left open.
- Thousand seed mass: not given by the source; left open (unlike `Tanja` and
  `Arola`, which both have source-backed figures).
