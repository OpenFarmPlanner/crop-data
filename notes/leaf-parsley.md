## Short Description
- Leaf parsley (`Petersilie, Blatt`) is `Petroselinum crispum`, grown for its
  leaves. This note covers leaf parsley only, not root parsley
  (`Petersilie, Wurzel` / Hamburg parsley, `Petroselinum crispum var.
  tuberosum`), which is a separate crop with a different harvest form
  (storage root) and its own crop-calendar logic; root parsley is not covered
  here and would need its own general crop note if researched.
- Leaf parsley is officially documented with two leaf types, flat/smooth
  ("glatt") and curly ("kraus"), which official German variety-trial
  publications (Bavarian
  [LWG Veitshöchheim](https://www.lwg.bayern.de/gartenakademie-infoschriften)
  and Rhineland-Palatinate
  [DLR Rheinpfalz](https://www.dlr.rlp.de/Gemuesebau), publishing in the
  "Versuche im deutschen Gartenbau" series) treat as a "Typ" (type) column
  within one crop entry, not as separate crops. See "Identity And Mapping
  Note" below for why this note follows that structure.
- Biennial grown as an annual for leaf harvest; moderate nutrient demand,
  even moisture, slow and somewhat unreliable germination.
- Growth duration: strongly season-dependent; left open at the general-crop
  level (see below) because sources give very different day counts for spring
  vs. summer sowings and for first vs. later cuts.

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow into a fine, moist seedbed; germination is slow
  (2-4 weeks) and uneven, so pre-soaking or a marker crop is common practice.
  Successional sowing keeps a continuous leaf supply.
- Sowing: about March/April to early September, in successions.
- Sowing depth: about 0.5-1.5 cm (sources vary in this range).

- Spacing: about 15-30 cm between rows (commercial figures cluster around 20
  cm), thinned to about 5-10 cm within the row; a stand density of about 40
  plants/m² is reported for commercial leaf-parsley cultivation
  ([Hortipendium - Petersilie Erwerbsanbau](https://www.hortipendium.de/Petersilie_Erwerbsanbau)).
- Site: sun to partial shade; humus-rich, evenly moist, free-draining soil.

## Harvest & Use
- Cut leaves once the plant is well established, always leaving the growing
  point intact for regrowth. Commercial sources give a first cut around 85-110
  days after sowing depending on season (faster in summer, slower in spring),
  with a second cut about 30-40 days after the first
  ([Hortipendium - Petersilie Erwerbsanbau](https://www.hortipendium.de/Petersilie_Erwerbsanbau)).
- Fresh use dominates; also frozen or dried, though curly types in particular
  lose aroma on drying.

## Notes
- Typ (glatt/kraus) is a leaf-shape and market-preference trait, not a
  cultivation-relevant split for spacing or sowing depth in the general
  sources used here; both types are grown the same way agronomically.

## Identity And Mapping Note
- This project's standing rule is that Kultur should always be the lowest,
  most specific cultivation-relevant level, never a genus-level umbrella
  term. Glatt and kraus parsley are botanically distinct cultivar groups, and
  in isolation that could argue for two separate Kulturen (as is done for
  Staudensellerie vs. Knollensellerie, which differ in harvest form and
  calendar logic).
- Here, however, the authoritative German variety-trial sources (LWG
  Veitshöchheim/Bayern and DLR Rheinpfalz's "Versuche im deutschen Gartenbau"
  series) publish glatt and kraus leaf-parsley varieties, including the four
  named varieties in this batch, together in one crop's variety-trial table,
  with "Typ" (glatt/kraus) as a column, not as two separate crop entries.
  Agronomically, sowing, spacing, and harvest logic do not differ between the
  two types in the sources reviewed.
- This note therefore keeps "Petersilie, Blatt" (leaf parsley) as ONE crop
  (Kultur), with glatt/kraus documented as a Typ attribute on each variety
  note, per the explicit instruction for this research batch. A retailer's
  shop-category structure lacking sub-categories would not by itself justify
  this decision; it is the trial-publication source structure that justifies
  treating glatt/kraus as a type within one crop rather than as two crops.
- This is flagged explicitly as a mapping decision rather than a silent
  choice, since it deviates from the general "narrowest reusable crop
  identity" preference and instead follows a documented type/crop precedent
  from the trial sources.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: left open for the general crop. The 85-110 day range for a
  first cut, and roughly 30-40 days for the following cut, varies strongly
  with sowing season; a single general value would be falsely precise. See
  each variety note for concrete, source- or inference-based values.
- Harvest window: left open for the same reason.
- Yield: left open. No reliable leaf-yield-per-m² source was found for leaf
  parsley.
