## Short Description
- Pak choi is `Brassica rapa` subsp. `chinensis`, a fast-growing Asian
  Brassica leaf vegetable grown for its thickened leaf midribs and tender
  leaves, harvested as loose rosettes or small heads.
- High nutrient demand (heavy feeder); needs consistently moist,
  nutrient-rich soil.
- Growth duration: about 50-70 days to full size depending on variety and
  harvest form, or about 4 weeks after transplanting for a baby-leaf
  harvest; see `Planning Value Derivations`.

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: pre-cultivate under glass for an earlier crop, then
  transplant out.
- Sowing: from about March under glass.
- Outdoor planting: transplant once seedlings are established; exact timing
  depends on the sowing batch.

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow outdoors once day length shortens after the
  summer solstice but conditions are still warm; pak choi is day-length
  sensitive and readily bolts if sown during lengthening/long days.
  Bolt-resistant varieties are commonly bred to widen the sowing window.
- Sowing: from about July outdoors.
- Sowing depth: 0.5-1 cm.

- Spacing: for transplants, about 30-40 cm between rows and 30 cm within the
  row for standard heads (wider, about 50 x 40 cm, for larger heads; closer,
  about 40 x 30-35 cm, for smaller heads); for direct sowing, about 30-40 cm
  between rows and 20-25 cm within the row; for a baby-leaf harvest, a close
  15 x 15 cm spacing is used instead.
- Site: sun to partial shade; nutrient-rich, evenly moist soil. As a heavy
  feeder, poor soil should be enriched with mature compost before sowing.

## Harvest & Use
- Mini/small pak choi types are harvested about 50-60 days after sowing;
  larger types take about 70 days. A baby-leaf harvest is possible about 4
  weeks after transplanting.

## Notes
- Germination takes about 5-12 days at 15-20 °C soil temperature.
- Day-length sensitivity is the main scheduling constraint: sowing while
  days are still lengthening (spring) risks premature bolting unless a
  bolt-resistant variety is used.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: left open as a single general value because sources give
  clearly different figures for different harvest forms: about 50-60 days
  for mini pak choi, about 70 days for larger head types, and about 28 days
  (4 weeks) for a baby-leaf harvest. A future planning value should specify
  which harvest form it targets rather than blending these.
- Harvest window: left open; no reliable general source gives a specific
  day count.
- Yield: left open; no general source reviewed here gives a reliable kg/m²
  figure.
- Thousand kernel weight: left open; not reviewed in this research pass.
