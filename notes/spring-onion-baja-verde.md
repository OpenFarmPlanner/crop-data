## Short Description
- `Baja Verde` (breeder code PS 290311) is an F1 hybrid bunching onion bred by
  Seminis-Petoseed, introduced in 1999 and originally adapted to Southern
  California and Mexico growing conditions
  ([NC State - Vegetable Cultivar Descriptions, Onion A-L](https://cucurbitbreeding.wordpress.ncsu.edu/2016/06/02/onion-a-l/)).
- Bred for pink root disease resistance; grown for edible green tops and an
  immature, non-bulbing shaft rather than a storage bulb
  ([NC State - Vegetable Cultivar Descriptions, Onion A-L](https://cucurbitbreeding.wordpress.ncsu.edu/2016/06/02/onion-a-l/)).
- Fast-developing, heat-tolerant variety with intensely green foliage, marketed
  for summer cultivation
  ([Hartmann Brockhaus - Lauchzwiebel Baja Verde](https://www.hartmann-brockhaus.de/detail/Lauchzwiebel-Baja-Verde)).
- Growth duration: 70 days from direct sowing, reused from the general crop.
  A supplier page claims about 10 days earlier maturity than "standard
  varieties", but without a defined baseline; see `Planning Value Derivations`.
- Harvest window: 30 days, reused from the general crop.

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow into a fine seedbed; the variety is described as
  fast-developing and heat-tolerant, suiting successive summer sowings.
- Sowing: March to August.
- Sowing depth: no variety-specific figure found; see general crop note
  (about 1-2 cm).

- Spacing: no variety-specific figure found; see general crop note (about
  30-40 cm between rows, about 2.5 cm within the row after thinning).
- Site: no variety-specific soil/site guidance found; see general crop note.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest once shafts and green tops reach usable size; no variety-specific
  harvest window was found (the general 30-day value is reused).
- Especially suited to Asian-style raw or lightly cooked preparations because
  of its mild flavor and intense green color
  ([Hartmann Brockhaus - Lauchzwiebel Baja Verde](https://www.hartmann-brockhaus.de/detail/Lauchzwiebel-Baja-Verde)).

## Notes
- Identity question, documented explicitly: the sources found describe `Baja
  Verde` only as a "bunching onion," which in commercial seed trade usage
  most often means `Allium fistulosum`, but the term is also used loosely for
  bunching-type `Allium cepa` varieties and for interspecific `fistulosum` x
  `cepa` hybrids bred specifically for disease resistance traits such as pink
  root resistance (a resistance source associated with `Allium cepa`
  breeding lines). No source found gives an explicit botanical species or
  parentage for `Baja Verde` beyond "F1 hybrid bunching onion." This is left
  as an open identity question rather than silently assuming pure `Allium
  fistulosum`.
- Pink root resistance is a useful field note for growers in regions where
  this soil-borne disease is present, but no Central European trial source
  was found confirming performance under local conditions; the variety's
  origin trial region (Southern California/Mexico) differs climatically.

## Comparison With General Crop Data
- Species/identity: the general crop note (`spring-onion.md`) documents that
  the "Lauchzwiebel" category spans both `Allium fistulosum` and bunching-type
  `Allium cepa`. `Baja Verde`'s exact species could not be confirmed from the
  sources checked; this is an open identity question at the variety level,
  not a silent inheritance of the general note's `fistulosum` framing.
- Sowing window: `Baja Verde` sources give March-August, close to the general
  crop's March-July-plus-autumn window; the extension into August is treated
  as variety-specific rather than assumed equal, since no autumn/overwintering
  sowing was mentioned for this variety.
- Sowing depth, spacing, and site requirements: no variety-specific values
  were found; these are intentionally reused from the general crop note
  because no `Baja Verde`-specific source contradicts them and no reliable
  variety-specific alternative was found.
- Growth duration: general crop 70 days; `Baja Verde` 70 days, intentionally
  reused (changed from an earlier 60). The Seminis-derived earliness claim
  (about 10 days earlier than "standard varieties") was confirmed on one
  reseller page, but it names no baseline and no absolute day count, and
  other Seminis-derived text (search summary only) gives a maturity of 100
  days, i.e. a full-maturity count that is not comparable with the general
  first-usable-harvest value. No variety-specific figure that fits the general
  value's definition exists, and none contradicts it.
- Harvest window: no variety-specific figure found; the general 30-day value
  is intentionally reused. The heat tolerance and low bulbing tendency could
  extend usable time in summer, but no source quantifies this, and no
  contradicting variety-specific source was found.
