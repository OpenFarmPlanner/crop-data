## Short Description
- `Kirschtomate` (cherry tomato) is `Solanum lycopersicum`. It covers small,
  round fruit (roughly 10-30 g), typically high in sugar and held in dense
  clusters on long trusses, eaten fresh/whole rather than sliced or cooked
  down. Confirmed via [ReinSaat](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/EN/tomaten_paradeiser/),
  which lists cherry tomatoes as its own subcategory.
- `Tomate`/`Tomato` alone is a generic culinary umbrella term for
  `Solanum lycopersicum`, not a valid Kultur value in this project. Siblings:
  [`Fleischtomate`](beefsteak-tomato.md) (beefsteak/oxheart, large slicing
  fruit), [`Soßentomate`](sauce-tomato.md) (Roma/San-Marzano-type paste
  fruit), `Kirschtomate` (this note), and [`Salattomate`](salad-tomato.md)
  (round salad/slicing fruit). Other fruit-size classes such as
  `Cocktailtomate` may exist botanically but are not documented in this
  batch of notes.
- Indeterminate, staked/trellised growth habit typical for garden cherry
  tomato varieties; some can grow quite tall (up to 2 m).
- Growth duration: left open here; the one variety researched so far
  (`Philovita`) gives a sowing/planting window but no concrete
  days-to-harvest figure, so a general value would be falsely precise.

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: Sow indoors in trays, plant out after the last frost with a
  support structure/stake, since cherry tomato plants can grow tall and
  carry heavy fruit clusters.
- Sowing: 02-01 to 04-30
- Outdoor planting: from mid-May, staked/trellised

- Spacing: 80 cm between rows; 50 cm within the row (general range, based
  on the `Philovita` source; see variety note for detail)
- Site: full sun, warm and sheltered; deep, humus-rich, evenly moist soil

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest by the truss or individually once fully colored; fruit continues
  ripening in clusters over an extended period.
- Eaten fresh, whole, in salads or as a snack; valued for high sugar content
  and aroma.

## Notes
- Cherry tomato plants can be vigorous and tall; sturdy staking is
  important.
- Modern hybrids are often bred with strong disease-resistance packages
  (see `Philovita` variety note for an example).
