## Short Description
- `Kirschtomate` (cherry tomato) is `Solanum lycopersicum`. It covers small,
  round fruit (roughly 10-30 g), typically high in sugar and held in dense
  clusters on long trusses, eaten fresh/whole rather than sliced or cooked
  down. Confirmed via [ReinSaat](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/EN/tomaten_paradeiser/),
  which lists cherry tomatoes as its own subcategory.
- `Tomate`/`Tomato` alone is a generic culinary umbrella term for
  `Solanum lycopersicum`, not a valid Kultur value in this project. Siblings:
  [`Fleischtomate`](beefsteak-tomato.md) (beefsteak/oxheart, large slicing
  fruit), [`Soßentomate`](sauce-tomato.md) (Roma/San-Marzano-type paste
  fruit), `Kirschtomate` (this note), and [`Salattomate`](salad-tomato.md)
  (round salad/slicing fruit). Other fruit-size classes such as
  `Cocktailtomate` may exist botanically but are not documented in this
  batch of notes.
- Indeterminate, staked/trellised growth habit typical for garden cherry
  tomato varieties; some can grow quite tall (up to 2 m).
- Growth duration: 65 days from transplanting to first harvest; harvest
  window 80 days; propagation duration 49 days (sowing to transplanting).
  These are representative planning values for a staked crop planted out
  in mid-May; earlier, heated or later plantings shift them (see `Planning
  Value Derivations`). Variety notes state whether they deviate.

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: Sow indoors in trays, plant out after the last frost with a
  support structure/stake, since cherry tomato plants can grow tall and
  carry heavy fruit clusters.
- Sowing: 02-01 to 04-30
- Sowing depth: about 0.5 cm (reused from the general `Fleischtomate` note's
  tomato-seed source).
- Outdoor planting: from mid-May, staked/trellised

- Spacing: 80 cm between rows; 50 cm within the row (general range, based
  on the `Philovita` source; see variety note for detail)
- Site: full sun, warm and sheltered; deep, humus-rich, evenly moist soil

## Germination
- Optimal germination temperature: 22-28 °C; minimum: 18 °C
- Germination time: 10-15 days
- Young plants should never be exposed to less than about 10 °C.
- Source: [ProSpecieRara - Angaben zur Pflanzenanzucht](https://cms.prospecierara.ch/assets/website/prospecierara.ch/Pflanzen/Wissen/20221214_Angaben_zur_Pflanzenzucht_by_ProSpecieRara.pdf) (row for tomato; applies to all tomato types).

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest by the truss or individually once fully colored; fruit continues
  ripening in clusters over an extended period.
- Eaten fresh, whole, in salads or as a snack; valued for high sugar content
  and aroma.

## Notes
- Cherry tomato plants can be vigorous and tall; sturdy staking is
  important.
- Modern hybrids are often bred with strong disease-resistance packages
  (see `Philovita` variety note for an example).

## Planning Value Derivations
- Definition: growth duration is counted from transplanting to the first
  harvest; harvest window is the length of the picking period; propagation
  duration is sowing to transplanting.
- Growth duration: 65 days. Source basis: [Garten-Querbeet - Wie lange brauchen Tomaten bis zur Ernte?](https://www.garten-querbeet.de/anbauen/wie-lange-brauchen-tomaten-bis-zur-ernte/) gives 50-90 days from
  flowering to ripe fruit depending on fruit size, about four weeks earlier
  harvest in a greenhouse than in the open, and first harvests from late June
  (early types) to early-to-late July (mid-season types); [Kiepenkerl - Tomaten](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/tomaten/) gives planting
  from mid to late May. Derivation: about 60-80 days from a mid-May planting
  to the first ripe fruit; small fruit ripen faster than large ones, so a value at the lower end of the range was chosen. Inferred, not a direct source value.
- Harvest window: 80 days. Source basis: field tomatoes ripen from about
  July and must be picked before the first frost in October ([Hausgarten.net - Tomatenanbau](https://www.hausgarten.net/tomatenanbau-tipps/)); long trusses ripen continuously and are picked repeatedly, so the harvest lasts longer than for large-fruited types, about 11-12 weeks.
  Inferred from the calendar wording, not a direct source value.
- Propagation duration: 49 days (7 weeks), the midpoint of the 6-8 weeks
  between sowing and outdoor planting given in the sowing section above.
  [Kiepenkerl - Tomaten](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/tomaten/) gives a longer calendar span (February sowing, mid-to-late May
  planting) but that includes waiting for frost-free planting time, not plant
  need; early sowings can be held for 10-12 weeks.
- Field/form constraint: whole integer days; weeks converted to days.
- Uncertainty: protected cultivation (tunnel, greenhouse) can start about four
  weeks earlier and harvest longer; determinate or very early varieties
  finish faster. Variety values should replace these where a source exists.
- Yield: 5.0 kg/m² (staked, single-stem, open-field to unheated-tunnel
  cultivation). Source basis: [Lubera - Selbstversorgung mit Tomaten](https://www.lubera.com/at/gartenbuch/selbstversorgung-mit-tomaten-p5729)
  gives 1.5-2.5 kg per plant as typical for cherry/cocktail tomatoes in
  hobby cultivation. Derivation: this note's own spacing (80 x 50 cm) gives
  a density of 2.5 plants/m²; 1.5-2.5 kg/plant x 2.5 plants/m² = 3.75-6.25
  kg/m², midpoint rounded to 5.0 kg/m². Inferred from a per-plant source, not
  a direct area-based figure. Institutional cluster-harvest trials for
  small/oval fruit report higher figures (about 7-12 kg/m²) under
  professional greenhouse conditions with different training systems; this
  general note keeps the more conservative home-garden-based figure and
  notes the higher institutional range as context, not as the planning
  value.
- Sowing depth: 0.5 cm, reused from the general `Fleischtomate` note's
  tomato-seed source.
