## Short Description
- `Fleischtomate` (beefsteak tomato) is `Solanum lycopersicum`. It is the
  meaty, large-fruited class of tomato, typically more than 150-200 g per
  fruit, with thick low-seed flesh and often ribbed or heart-shaped
  ("Ochsenherz"/oxheart) fruit. It is grown for slicing and fresh eating, not
  for sauce or preserving.
- `Tomate`/`Tomato` alone is a generic culinary umbrella term for
  `Solanum lycopersicum`, not a valid Kultur value in this project. Tomatoes
  are split here by fruit-size and use class into distinct Kulturen:
  `Fleischtomate` (this note; beefsteak/oxheart, large slicing fruit),
  [`Soßentomate`](sauce-tomato.md) (Roma/San-Marzano-type paste fruit),
  [`Kirschtomate`](cherry-tomato.md) (cherry, small sweet fruit), and
  [`Salattomate`](salad-tomato.md) (round salad/slicing fruit, including the
  truss/Strauchtomate growth habit). Other fruit-size classes such as
  `Cocktailtomate` may exist botanically but are not documented in this
  batch of notes.
- Indeterminate (vining), staked/trellised growth habit; warm-season crop
  that needs support and regular removal of side shoots ("Ausgeizen").
- Growth duration: left open here. Beefsteak varieties range from
  earlier-ripening modern hybrids to long-season heirloom oxheart types, and
  a single general figure would be falsely precise. See variety notes for
  concrete values.

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: Start indoors/in trays, then plant outdoors after the last
  frost once nights stay reliably above about 10 °C. Staking or a trellis is
  required for the indeterminate growth habit.
- Sowing: 02-01 to 04-15
- Outdoor planting: 6-8 weeks after sowing, from mid-May

- Spacing: 70-90 cm between rows; 45-60 cm within the row (general range for
  staked beefsteak varieties; see variety notes for source-specific values)
- Site: full sun, warm and wind-sheltered location; deep, humus-rich,
  evenly moist but well-drained soil

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest when fruit is fully colored and gives slightly to gentle pressure.
- Used fresh: sliced for sandwiches and salads, valued for thick, low-seed
  flesh rather than for cooking down into sauce.

## Notes
- Prone to blossom end rot under uneven watering or calcium deficiency,
  more visible on large-fruited types because of the high fruit-wall mass.
- Needs consistent watering, staking, and removal of side shoots for
  indeterminate varieties.
- Many commercial beefsteak hybrids are bred with resistance packages
  (fungal wilts, nematodes); heirloom oxheart types typically have fewer
  built-in resistances and may need more disease management.
