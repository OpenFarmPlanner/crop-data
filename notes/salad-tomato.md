## Short Description
- `Salattomate` (salad/slicing tomato) is `Solanum lycopersicum`. It covers
  round, medium-to-large fruit (roughly 100-200 g) not otherwise assigned to
  the beefsteak, sauce, or cherry classes - grown for fresh eating and
  slicing. This Kultur also covers the truss/Strauchtomate growth habit
  (fruit harvested and often sold on the cluster) as a cultivation-type note
  rather than a separate Kultur, since
  [ReinSaat](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/EN/tomaten_paradeiser/) has no
  distinct "Strauchtomate" subcategory.
- `Tomate`/`Tomato` alone is a generic culinary umbrella term for
  `Solanum lycopersicum`, not a valid Kultur value in this project. Siblings:
  [`Fleischtomate`](beefsteak-tomato.md) (beefsteak/oxheart, large slicing
  fruit), [`Soßentomate`](sauce-tomato.md) (Roma/San-Marzano-type paste
  fruit), [`Kirschtomate`](cherry-tomato.md) (cherry, small sweet fruit), and
  `Salattomate` (this note). Other fruit-size classes such as
  `Cocktailtomate` may exist botanically but are not documented in this
  batch of notes.
- Indeterminate, staked/trellised growth habit for the varieties researched
  so far.
- Growth duration: 70 days from transplanting to first harvest; harvest
  window 70 days; propagation duration 49 days (sowing to transplanting).
  These are representative planning values for a staked crop planted out
  in mid-May; earlier, heated or later plantings shift them (see `Planning
  Value Derivations`). Variety notes state whether they deviate.

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: Sow indoors in trays, plant out after the last frost with a
  support structure/stake.
- Sowing: 02-01 to 04-15
- Outdoor planting: from mid-May, staked/trellised

- Spacing: 60-90 cm between rows; 45-70 cm within the row (general range
  aggregated across the varieties researched so far; see variety notes for
  source-specific values)
- Site: full sun, warm and sheltered; deep, humus-rich, evenly moist soil

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest when fully colored; can be picked as loose fruit or, for
  truss-type varieties, as a whole cluster.
- Eaten fresh, sliced for salads and general kitchen use.

## Notes
- The `Salattomate` Kultur is used here as a "catch-all" for round,
  medium-large slicing tomatoes that are not beefsteak-, sauce-, or
  cherry-type; where a variety's exact class was uncertain (see `Sacher
  Braun` and `Lorenzo`), that uncertainty is documented explicitly in the
  variety note rather than silently assumed.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Definition: growth duration is counted from transplanting to the first
  harvest; harvest window is the length of the picking period; propagation
  duration is sowing to transplanting.
- Growth duration: 70 days. Source basis: [Garten-Querbeet - Wie lange brauchen Tomaten bis zur Ernte?](https://www.garten-querbeet.de/anbauen/wie-lange-brauchen-tomaten-bis-zur-ernte/) gives 50-90 days from
  flowering to ripe fruit depending on fruit size, about four weeks earlier
  harvest in a greenhouse than in the open, and first harvests from late June
  (early types) to early-to-late July (mid-season types); [Kiepenkerl - Tomaten](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/tomaten/) gives planting
  from mid to late May. Derivation: about 60-80 days from a mid-May planting
  to the first ripe fruit; medium-sized round fruit sit in the middle of the range. Inferred, not a direct source value.
- Harvest window: 70 days. Source basis: field tomatoes ripen from about
  July and must be picked before the first frost in October ([Hausgarten.net - Tomatenanbau](https://www.hausgarten.net/tomatenanbau-tipps/)); round salad fruit are picked over about ten weeks between mid-July and late September.
  Inferred from the calendar wording, not a direct source value.
- Propagation duration: 49 days (7 weeks), the midpoint of the 6-8 weeks
  between sowing and outdoor planting given in the sowing section above.
  [Kiepenkerl - Tomaten](https://www.kiepenkerl.de/kulturanleitungen/tomaten/) gives a longer calendar span (February sowing, mid-to-late May
  planting) but that includes waiting for frost-free planting time, not plant
  need; early sowings can be held for 10-12 weeks.
- Field/form constraint: whole integer days; weeks converted to days.
- Uncertainty: protected cultivation (tunnel, greenhouse) can start about four
  weeks earlier and harvest longer; determinate or very early varieties
  finish faster. Variety values should replace these where a source exists.
