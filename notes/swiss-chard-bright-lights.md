## Short Description
- `Bright Lights` (given in the source task as "Bright Light"; see `Notes`
  for this naming discrepancy) is a well-known multicolour-stem Swiss chard
  variety/seed mix (`Beta vulgaris` subsp. `vulgaris`), bred for high colour
  intensity and variety rather than for a single stem colour.
- Stems appear in green, red, white, pink, and yellow within one seed batch;
  leaves range from dark green to reddish-brown. Colours are reported to
  hold up during cooking. Plants reach about 80 cm in height.
- Growth duration: harvest is given as about 60-90 days after sowing by one
  retailer source; see `Planning Value Derivations`.

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow outdoors; the variety is usable both as a
  cut-and-come-again leaf chard and, once mature, as a stalk vegetable,
  spanning both type branches described in the general note.
- Sowing: April to July.
- Sowing depth: not given by the source reviewed; left open, matching the
  general crop.

- Spacing: about 25 cm within the row, 30 cm between rows, per the retailer
  source used here.
- Site: full sun (source-specific guidance; the general crop allows sun to
  partial shade).

## Harvest & Use
- Two harvest approaches are described: cut about one third of the leaf
  mass repeatedly while leaving the heart to regrow (leaf/cut-chard style),
  or harvest mature stalks whole for stewing or steaming alongside the
  leaves (stalk-chard style). The variety is explicitly dual-purpose across
  both type branches noted in the general `Mangold` note.
- Decorative/ornamental value is also commonly cited for this variety
  (colourful stalks in beds or containers), noted here as a practical
  cultivation aside rather than a structured field.

## Notes
- Naming discrepancy: the task/source name for this variety was given as
  "Bright Light" (singular), but every seed-trade source found during this
  research uses the plural "Bright Lights". This is documented explicitly
  as a possible naming variant rather than silently corrected; the file is
  named `swiss-chard-bright-lights.md` to match the sources, and the
  singular form is preserved above as the originally requested name.
- Because this variety spans both the leaf/cut and stalk harvest styles
  described in the general note, its own values (spacing, harvest days)
  should not be read as representative of a single-purpose leaf-only or
  stalk-only chard variety.

## Comparison With General Crop Data
- Crop identity matches the general `Mangold`/Swiss chard note: both are
  `Beta vulgaris` subsp. `vulgaris`, leaf/petiole group.
- Sowing window (April-July) is close to, but slightly wider than, the
  general crop's April-June window; the extra July option is documented
  here as a variety/retailer-specific extension, not silently merged into
  the general note.
- Spacing (25 cm within row x 30 cm between rows) sits within the general
  crop's leaf/cut chard range (25-35 cm between rows x 35 cm within row) but
  is narrower within the row than the general figure; documented as a
  variety-specific deviation from one retailer source rather than assumed
  equal.
- Growth duration (60-90 days from this source) is a variety/retailer-level
  figure; the general crop leaves growth duration open, so this is not a
  reuse of a general value but the only concrete figure found for this
  crop in this research pass. It is not necessarily representative of the
  stalk-harvest style, since 60-90 days is closer to a leaf-harvest timing.
- Yield and thousand kernel weight: no variety-specific figures were found;
  both remain open, matching the general crop's open status. No general
  planning value exists yet to reuse either.
