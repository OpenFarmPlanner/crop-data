## Short Description
- `Melville F1` (also listed under the breeder code `SV 5044 VC`) is an
  early to mid-early maturing spinach variety (`Spinacia oleracea`), suited
  to spring/early-summer and autumn sowings.
- Thick, round leaves with notably uniform plant development; relatively
  late bolting for its maturity class; good post-harvest shelf life; bred
  for resistance to powdery mildew (pathotypes Pfs 1-15+17).
- Growth duration: 50 days from direct sowing and harvest window 21 days;
  both are intentionally reused from the general crop because no variety-specific
  day count was found (see `Comparison With General Crop Data`). No
  propagation duration; direct sown.

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow in rows; thin lightly. Pre-cultivation is unusual
  for spinach, and no variety-specific deviation from this was found for
  `Melville F1`.
- Sowing: about February to April for late-spring harvest, and about August
  to October for autumn, winter, and early-spring harvest, reused from the
  general crop; the source describes the variety as suited to "spring -
  early summer and autumn" plantings, consistent with these windows.
- Sowing depth: 2-3 cm, reused from the general crop; no variety-specific
  figure was found.

- Spacing: about 20-25 cm between rows, a few cm within the row, reused from
  the general crop; no variety-specific figure was found.
- Site: sun to light shade; cool conditions; humus-rich, moisture-retentive
  soil, reused from the general crop. The variety's relatively late-bolting
  trait may make it comparatively more forgiving of a longer-day, warmer
  spring sowing window than a bolt-prone variety, but this is not converted
  into a separate sowing-window value here.

## Harvest & Use
- Cut whole young plants or pick outer leaves, as for the general crop.
  Good post-harvest shelf life is reported for this variety, relevant for
  handling after cutting rather than for the harvest method itself.

## Notes
- Powdery mildew resistance (Pfs 1-15+17) is a variety-specific disease
  trait with no general crop equivalent; documented here as practical
  cultivation guidance rather than a structured field.
- "Relatively late bolting" is a qualitative breeder claim; no numeric
  day-to-bolt figure was found, so it is not converted into a planning
  value.
