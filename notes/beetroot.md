## Short Description
- Beetroot (`Rote Bete`, `Rote Rübe`) is `Beta vulgaris` subsp. `vulgaris`
  (Conditiva Group), grown for its swollen, edible root; leaves are also
  edible.
- Biennial grown as an annual. Undemanding but benefits from even moisture
  (uneven watering can cause internal ring hardening or cracking) and a
  brassica-free rotation slot (it is not a brassica).
- Growth duration: about 120-150 days from sowing to full-size harvest is a
  commonly cited general range; early bunching types and baby-beet harvest are
  much faster (see variety notes for concrete values).

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow in rows; thin seedlings after germination to the
  final in-row spacing, since each beet "seed" is a multi-germ cluster in
  non-monogerm varieties.
- Sowing: mid-April to July.
- Sowing depth: about 2 cm.

- Spacing: about 25-50 cm between rows, 5-10 cm within the row after thinning
  (wider spacing for later, larger storage roots; tighter for bunching types).
- Site: sun to partial shade; humus-rich, evenly moist, well-drained soil.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest from about 5-6 weeks after sowing for baby beets, through 3-4 months
  for full-size roots; can be left in the ground into autumn in mild sites or
  lifted for storage before hard frost.
- Eaten cooked, pickled, or raw (thinly sliced); leaves usable like spinach.

## Notes
- Beet color/pattern (deep red, yellow/orange, or ringed red-and-white) is a
  variety trait, not a general-crop trait; document it explicitly per variety
  (see `notes/beetroot-golden-eye.md`, `notes/beetroot-chioggia.md`,
  `notes/beetroot-alvro-mono.md`).

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: 130 days used as the whole-day midpoint of the commonly
  cited 120-150 day general range from garden sources
  ([Ackerhelden](https://ackerhelden.de/blogs/bio-garten-blog/rote-bete-anbau-pflege-ernte),
  [erntefibel](https://www.erntefibel.de/gem%C3%BCse-a-z/rote-beete-1/aussaat-rote-beete/)).
  Documented as an inferred midpoint, not a direct single-source value; early
  and baby-beet harvest deviate strongly below this.
- Yield: 4-6 kg/m² is the commonly cited general range; kept as a range because
  no single authoritative commercial figure was found, and because yield
  depends strongly on final root size (bunching vs. storage harvest).
