## Short Description
- `Chioggia` (`Tonda di Chioggia`) is an old Italian beetroot variety, `Beta
  vulgaris` subsp. `vulgaris`, matched to the general crop note
  `notes/beetroot.md`. Named after the Italian city of Chioggia in the Venice
  lagoon.
- Distinguishing trait: alternating red-and-white concentric rings inside the
  root (the source of the German name `Ringelbete`), with a bright light-red
  exterior. Retail and cultivation sources describe the taste as sweeter and
  less earthy than standard red beetroot, and the roots bleed less red juice
  when cut. The ring pattern fades toward salmon color when cooked.
- Growth duration: about 100 days from germination to first harvest is the
  variety-specific value used here (see `Planning Value Derivations`).

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow in rows; thin after germination. ReinSaat's variety
  page gives the widest sowing window found for this crop group.
- Sowing: April to July.
- Sowing depth: no variety-specific depth source found; the general crop's
  2 cm value is reused.

- Spacing: 30-50 cm between rows, 5-10 cm within the row, taken directly from
  [ReinSaat](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/EN/rote_ruebe/tonda_di_chioggia/).
- Site: sun to partial shade; humus-rich, evenly moist, well-drained soil
  (general crop guidance; no variety-specific deviation found).

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest about 100 days after germination for a May sowing, roughly a July
  harvest; can also be pulled earlier as a smaller salad beet.
- Best used raw (thinly sliced into salads) to show the ring pattern, which
  fades when cooked; also usable cooked like standard beetroot.

## Comparison With General Crop Data
- Crop identity matches the general `Rote Bete` / beetroot note: both are
  `Beta vulgaris` subsp. `vulgaris`.
- Ring pattern and color are the variety's defining deviation from the general
  crop: alternating red-and-white rings instead of solid red flesh. Documented
  explicitly because it affects culinary use (best raw) even though
  cultivation needs are similar to the general crop.
- Growth duration (100 days) is documented directly from a variety-specific
  source and is somewhat shorter than the general crop's inferred 130-day
  midpoint; this deviation is kept rather than overridden by the general
  value, since it comes from a direct source statement ("about 100 days from
  germination to first harvest").
- Spacing is documented directly from a variety-specific source (30-50 x
  5-10 cm), matching the general crop's range exactly; no deviation.
- Sowing depth: no variety-specific figure found; the general crop's 2 cm
  value is intentionally reused, since no contradicting variety-specific
  source was found.
- Yield: no variety-specific figure was found; the general crop's 4-6 kg/m²
  range is intentionally reused, since no contradicting variety-specific
