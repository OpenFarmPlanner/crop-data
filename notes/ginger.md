## Short Description
- Ginger (`Ingwer`, `Zingiber officinale`) is a tropical perennial grown for
  its fleshy rhizome, treated in Central Europe as an annual crop in
  greenhouse or (mostly unheated) foil tunnel; open-field cultivation works
  only in mild regions.
- Warm, humid, partly shaded site; very long cultivation cycle.
- Growth duration: 170 days from planting out to first mature harvest,
  propagation (pre-sprouting) 56 days, harvest window 60 days; planning values
  for protected Central European cultivation, see `Planning Value
  Derivations`. These differ strongly from tropical field values (about
  10 months in the field, harvest from December in Hawaii).

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: cut a healthy rhizome into pieces of about 40-70 g with 2-4
  developed buds, let the cuts dry and heal for 2-7 days, then lay the pieces
  about 10 x 10 cm apart in a substrate layer of about 5 cm in trays at
  24-28 °C; keep moist but not wet. This follows the Austrian organic
  growing guide by [FiBL Austria](https://www.fibl.org/fileadmin/documents/shop/1804-anbauratgeber-ingwer.pdf),
  which summarizes trials at LWG Bamberg/Veitshöchheim and the Versuchsanstalt
  Wies.
- Start: March is named as the best start of germination in the guide above;
  heated or mixed operations may start in January-February. Clean planting
  material is a health issue (soil- and seed-borne bacterial wilt), so
  supermarket rhizomes are a risk.
- Planting out: April to May into the tunnel (about 6-8 weeks after
  starting, plants 20-50 cm with 2-3 shoots); greenhouse planting is possible
  once soil is at least 12 °C. Outdoors only after mid-May (Ice Saints) and
  where nights stay warm.
- Planting depth: 5 cm of soil cover, see `Planning Value Derivations`
  (conflict resolved).
- Spacing: 60 cm between rows and 40 cm in the row (about 4 plants/m²) was
  used at Wies; protected-cultivation trials range from 4 to 11 plants/m²,
  and 5.5 plants/m² was as good as 6.8 plants/m² at Bamberg. Planting into
  furrows makes hilling easier.
- Site: warm, humid, shaded against strong sun (shade net or paint; young
  plants burn above about 32 °C), loose humus-rich soil, even moisture
  without waterlogging (about 500-1200 mm per season).

## Harvest & Use
- Central Europe, protected cultivation: mature harvest from mid/late
  September to November/December, before frost reaches the rhizome; lift
  plants step by step for the market. Young, thin-skinned ginger can be taken
  from August, but yields are then much lower (one New Hampshire tunnel trial:
  221 g/plant on 1 September versus 466 g/plant on 1 November).
- Tropical field (Hawaii): harvest begins in December after about
  10 months; not transferable to Central Europe.
- Part of the rhizome can be kept as propagation material at 12-14 °C
  (not below 12 °C).
- Used fresh or dried as a spice and tea.

## Notes
- Calendar values: see `Planning Value Derivations`.
- Yield: about 3 kg/m² fresh rhizome for protected cultivation, see
  `Planning Value Derivations`.
- Crop family Zingiberaceae; the guide cites about 250 kg N, 55 kg P and
  100 kg K per ha for ginger in general; nutrient demand class not mapped.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Definitions: propagation duration is the start of the rhizome pieces
  (pre-sprouting) until planting out; growth duration is counted from
  planting out to first mature harvest.
- Two regimes must not be mixed. Tropical field: [University of Hawaii CTAHR](https://www3.ctahr.hawaii.edu/oc/freepubs/pdf/CFS-GIN-3A.pdf)
  gives planting from March, an optimum season of 10 months and harvest from
  December. Central European tunnel or greenhouse: the FiBL guide and the
  Austrian producer reports below; the season is much shorter and yields are
  lower.
- Growth duration: 170 days. Planting out in April-May and mature harvest
  from about early October gives about 150-180 days; the Bamberg trial
  harvested on 7 October 2021 once soil fell to 13 °C. The guide names a
  first (young) harvest in August, i.e. about 100-120 days. Austrian
  reports say 5-6 months ([Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Ingwer_Erwerbsanbau))
  and 5-7 months from May planting ([Ja! Natuerlich](https://www.janatuerlich.at/magazin/exotisch-und-aus-oesterreich-bio-ingwer/)).
  170 days is the middle of this range for planting around mid-April;
  inferred, not a direct source value. Earlier value was 180.
- Propagation duration: 56 days. The FiBL guide states about 50 days average
  germination and 6-8 weeks (42-56 days) in total for young plant raising;
  the upper end is used. Direct source range, day count chosen from it.
- Harvest window: 60 days. Sources give mid/late September to November/
  December (FiBL) and early/mid October to year end ([Ja! Natuerlich](https://www.janatuerlich.at/magazin/exotisch-und-aus-oesterreich-bio-ingwer/)),
  i.e. 60-90 days; a step-wise harvest as needed is recommended. 60 days is
  a conservative planning value (October-November); inferred.
- Yield: 3 kg/m² (protected cultivation). FiBL cites 2-5 kg/m² depending on
  harvest date and site, Bamberg heated 2.9-3.9 kg/m² (5.5-6.8 and 4.1
  plants/m²), and an unheated 2021 tunnel trial at Bamberg 1.8-4.1 kg/m²
  across seven origins (marketable share lower). Tropical field about
  4.5 kg/m² (CTAHR: about 18,100 kg/acre, i.e. 18,144 kg per 4,047 m²;
  40,000 lb/acre average), not transferable. The planning value is a rounded
  middle of tunnel results; variety-independent.
- Planting depth conflict: 2 cm (one garden source) versus 5 cm (Plantura).
  Extension guidance is deeper: CTAHR covers pieces with 5-10 cm of soil in
  furrows and hills them later. 5 cm is kept as the planting-out value for
  young transplants; further hilling by 10 cm or more is recommended by FiBL.
  2 cm is discarded as garden-scale and not supported by extension sources.
- Start date conflict: January-February (garden sources) versus March
  (FiBL, first pre-sprouting date; Bamberg trials planted in March in a
  heated tunnel). Both are valid: January-February needs heat; for unheated
  tunnels start in March and plant in April-May.
- Field/form constraint: whole days.
- Uncertainty: moderate for yield and dates in tunnels; large outdoors.
