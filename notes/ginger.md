## Short Description
- Ginger (`Ingwer`, `Zingiber officinale`) is a tropical perennial grown for
  its fleshy rhizome, treated in Central Europe as an annual (or overwintered
  dry indoors) crop in pots, greenhouse, or tunnel; open-field cultivation
  works only in mild regions.
- Warm, humid, bright site; very long cultivation cycle.
- Growth duration: 180 days from planting out to first harvest, propagation
  (pre-sprouting) 56 days, harvest window 30 days; inferred planning values
  for protected cultivation, see `Planning Value Derivations`.

## Template Adaptation
- The template offers transplants (sowing), direct sowing, and overwintering
  crop. Ginger is planted from rhizome pieces, not seed. The closest mapping
  is `Transplants`: the pre-sprouted rhizome piece plays the role of the
  transplant, and "sowing" means starting the pieces. This is a mapping
  compromise and is flagged in `Research Status`.

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: cut a healthy rhizome into pieces of about 4-5 cm with at
  least one, ideally 2-3 buds; a warm-water soak and a few weeks of warmth are
  needed until shoots appear
  ([meine-ernte.de](https://www.meine-ernte.de/pflanzen-a-z/gemuese/ingwer/),
  [Plantura](https://www.plantura.garden/gemuese/ingwer/ingwer-pflanzenportrait)).
- Start: January to February indoors; one source names this as the best start
  because of the long cycle.
- Planting out: protected cultivation from March at the earliest; outdoors
  only after mid-May and where temperatures stay above about 12 °C.
- Planting depth: about 2 cm of soil cover (one source) to 5 cm (Plantura);
  not resolved.

- Spacing: no reliable field spacing found (sources address pots); left open.
- Site: sunniest, warmest spot, loose humus-rich soil, even moisture without
  waterlogging.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest from October, when foliage has mostly yellowed; part of the rhizome
  can be kept as propagation material stored dry at about 10 °C.
- Used fresh or dried as a spice and tea.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Definitions: propagation duration is the start of the rhizome pieces
  (pre-sprouting) until planting out; growth duration is counted from
  planting out to first harvest.
- Growth duration: 180 days. [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Ingwer_Erwerbsanbau)
  gives a cultivation duration of about 5-6 months in warm protected
  cultivation; [Plantura](https://www.plantura.garden/gemuese/ingwer/ingwer-pflanzenportrait)
  and other garden guides describe 8-10 months in total, with tubers ripe
  after about 250 days, foliage yellowing after 8-9 months and harvest from
  October. 180 days is the upper end of the protected-cultivation figure and
  fits planting in March-April and harvest in October. Outdoors after
  mid-May the season is shorter (about 150 days), so results are less
  reliable there. Inferred, not a direct source value.
- Propagation duration: 56 days, inferred from the start in January-February
  and planting out from March to April; sources give no day count.
  Together with 180 days this totals about 236 days, consistent with the
  roughly 250 days quoted for maturity.
- Harvest window: 30 days, inferred. Harvest starts in October when foliage
  yellows; young rhizomes can be taken earlier, and roots must be lifted
  before cold weather. No source gives a day count.
- Field/form constraint: whole days.
- Uncertainty: large; depends on heating, start date and harvest stage.
