## Short Description
- Ginger (`Ingwer`, `Zingiber officinale`) is a tropical perennial grown for
  its fleshy rhizome, treated in Central Europe as an annual (or overwintered
  dry indoors) crop in pots, greenhouse, or tunnel; open-field cultivation
  works only in mild regions.
- Warm, humid, bright site; very long cultivation cycle.
- Growth duration: sources give roughly 9-10 months from planting to
  harvest, but outdoor timing depends on when protected pre-sprouting starts;
  left open as a planning value, see `Notes`.

## Template Adaptation
- The template offers transplants (sowing), direct sowing, and overwintering
  crop. Ginger is planted from rhizome pieces, not seed. The closest mapping
  is `Transplants`: the pre-sprouted rhizome piece plays the role of the
  transplant, and "sowing" means starting the pieces. This is a mapping
  compromise and is flagged in `Research Status`.

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: cut a healthy rhizome into pieces of about 4-5 cm with at
  least one, ideally 2-3 buds; a warm-water soak and a few weeks of warmth are
  needed until shoots appear
  ([meine-ernte.de](https://www.meine-ernte.de/pflanzen-a-z/gemuese/ingwer/),
  [Plantura](https://www.plantura.garden/gemuese/ingwer/ingwer-pflanzenportrait)).
- Start: January to February indoors; one source names this as the best start
  because of the long cycle.
- Planting out: protected cultivation from March at the earliest; outdoors
  only after mid-May and where temperatures stay above about 12 °C.
- Planting depth: about 2 cm of soil cover (one source) to 5 cm (Plantura);
  not resolved.

- Spacing: no reliable field spacing found (sources address pots); left open.
- Site: sunniest, warmest spot, loose humus-rich soil, even moisture without
  waterlogging.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest from October, when foliage has mostly yellowed; part of the rhizome
  can be kept as propagation material stored dry at about 10 °C.
- Used fresh or dried as a spice and tea.

## Notes
- Calendar values left open: the long cycle depends on start date, heating,
  and harvest stage (young "fresh" vs. mature); a single growth duration would
  be falsely precise.
- Yield: no reliable kg/m² figure found; left open.
- Crop family Zingiberaceae; nutrient demand unclear, likely medium-high
  (regular organic fertilizing recommended); not verified.
