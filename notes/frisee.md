## Short Description
- Krause Endivie (Kultur name; English name Frisée with synonym "Curly
  endive"; German synonyms `Frisée`, `Endiviensalat`) is
  the curly, finely divided leaf type of `Cichorium endivia`, botanically
  `Cichorium endivia var. crispum` (see "Identity And Mapping Note" for the
  naming check). It forms a loose, flat to slightly upright rosette with a
  paler, partly self-blanching heart. It is grown mainly as a summer to autumn
  salad, transplanted after pre-cultivation.
- This note covers the curly type only. The smooth, broad-leaved type
  (Kultur `Glatte Endivie`, trade name Escariol) is a separate crop with its
  own note; sourced differences are listed under "Differences To Glatte
  Endivie (Escarole)".
- Annual salad crop, family Asteraceae (Korbblütler), same family as lettuce,
  chicory and radicchio. Medium nutrient demand, needs even water supply.
  Fresh use only; keeps for a shorter time than escarole after harvest.
- Growth duration: 49 days from transplanting to harvest (planning value,
  about 10-12 weeks from sowing in total). Harvest window: 14 days (inferred
  planning value). Both are derived, see Planning Value Derivations.
- Expected yield: 3.5 kg/m² (planning value from the German fertilization-
  regulation yield level for Frisée, see Planning Value Derivations).

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: sow in seed trays or press pots and keep the young plants
  warm; the seed is a light germinator and is only covered thinly. Plant out
  after about 3-5 weeks. Keep pre-cultivation warm (above about 17 degrees C)
  and avoid early cold spells after planting, because cool weather can trigger
  bolting. Successional sowings give a continuous supply.
- Sowing: 2026-05-01 to 2026-07-15 (main field crop; sources give early May to
  mid July for open-field pre-cultivation, June to mid July for the autumn
  crop). Earlier sowings (April) are only advisable under cover and with
  bolting-resistant varieties; later sowings (to about August, or October under
  protection) are reported by some sources but are not used as the planning
  window.
- Outdoor planting: 3-5 weeks after sowing (planning value 4 weeks).

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sowing is mentioned by seed companies (a higher seed
  quantity than for transplants), but the sources reviewed treat pre-
  cultivation as the standard method; direct sowing is therefore not the
  planning basis here.
- Sowing: 2026-05-15 to 2026-07-01 (indicative only, follows the transplant
  window shifted for field emergence).
- Sowing depth: about 1 cm, only lightly covered (sources 0.5-1 cm; one garden
  source 2-3 cm).

- Spacing: 30 cm between rows; 30 cm within the row (about 11 plants/m²).
  Sources give 30 x 30-40 cm for a named curly variety, and 33 x 33 cm or 38 x
  30 cm for endive in general (8-9 plants/m²).
- Site: full sun, moisture-retentive but well-drained, humus-rich soil with
  even water supply; avoid excess nitrogen, which promotes leaf-edge burn.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest whole heads once the rosette is well filled and the heart has
  developed a pale yellow colour, typically 10-12 weeks after sowing, mainly
  September to November for the summer and autumn plantings.
- Blanching: curly types are often blanched to enlarge the pale heart. Either
  tie the outer leaves together on dry days about two weeks before harvest, or
  cover the heads with a light-proof cover about one week before harvest.
  Modern self-blanching varieties need this less.
- Use: fresh salad; mild, more delicate taste than escarole. Shelf life after
  harvest is short (a few days).

## Notes
- Bolting: temperatures below about 17 degrees C during young-plant growth,
  particularly around 4 degrees C, and long days in spring promote bolting;
  warm pre-cultivation and bolting-resistant varieties reduce the risk. Very
  hot weather (above about 25 degrees C) is also reported to trigger flowering.
- Frost: sources disagree on whether curly or smooth endive is hardier; see
  Open source conflicts. Protect with fleece against early hard frost.
- Rotation: do not follow other chicory-family crops (radicchio, chicory,
  witloof); a break of 3-4 years on the same bed is recommended.
- Leaf-edge burn and rot in dense, wet heads are typical problems; wet weather
  and high nitrogen increase them.

## Differences To Glatte Endivie (Escarole)
Only sourced differences are listed; all values apply to Frisée first.
- Spacing and blanching: [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Endivien_Erwerbsanbau)
  states that curly types can be planted closer and need a longer blanching
  period for the yellow heart, while smooth types are planted wider (example
  38 x 30 cm), are ready sooner and need less blanching. A garden source
  ([Samen.de](https://samen.de/blog/unterschiede-zwischen-eskariol-und-frisee-welche-endivienart-passt-zu-ihrem-garten.html))
  gives the same spacing and duration for both types.
- Fertilization-regulation yield level and nitrogen need: Frisée 350 dt/ha and
  150 kg N/ha versus smooth-leaved endive 600 dt/ha and 190 kg N/ha
  ([DüV Annex 4 Table 4 as published by LKSH](https://www.lksh.de/fileadmin/PDFs/Landwirtschaft/Duengung/Stickstoffbedarfswertefuer_Gemuesekulturen_und_Erdbeeren.pdf)).
- Head form: curly type forms loose, flat rosettes; smooth type forms compact
  heads with dark outer and pale inner leaves.
- Storage: curly type keeps only a few days; smooth type keeps longer
  ([Gemüsekorb](https://www.gemuesekorb.info/salate/endivie.php),
  [Samen.de](https://samen.de/blog/unterschiede-zwischen-eskariol-und-frisee-welche-endivienart-passt-zu-ihrem-garten.html)).
- Frost: contradictory between sources (see Research Status).
- Sowing window: one garden source gives April-August for Frisée versus May-July
  for Escariol.
- Taste: curly type is finer and more salad-like, smooth type more bitter.

## Identity And Mapping Note
- Botanical name: `Cichorium endivia var. crispum` is the name recorded by
  [EPPO Global Database](https://gd.eppo.int/taxon/CICEC) (authority Lamarck)
  and listed by [World Flora Online](https://www.worldfloraonline.org/taxon/wfo-0001040371)
  (authority given as Mill.) Lam.; the older name `Cichorium crispum` is treated
  as a synonym ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cichorium_endivia)).
  The authority citation differs between sources (Lam., DC., (Mill.) Lam.), and
  the Plants of the World Online page could not be read (access error), so the
  current accepted rank (variety vs. subspecies vs. cultivar group) was not
  confirmed there. The variety name is used as working botanical name.
- Naming conflict: EPPO lists an English name for this variety that is the
  same as the name commonly used for the smooth type in German ("Winterendivie"
  is also used for Escariol by a German source). The name "Winterendivie" is
  therefore not used as a synonym here. In US English the bare word "endive"
  refers to Belgian endive (`Cichorium intybus`); only "Curly endive" and
  "Frisée" are used as English names.
- Split decision: the previous single crop "Endiviensalat" (`Cichorium endivia`)
  mixed two botanical varieties with different cultivation logic (spacing,
  blanching effort, frost tolerance, storage, N need and yield level in the
  DüV table). Following the lowest-cultivation-level rule, Krause Endivie and Glatte
  Endivie are documented as two crops (descriptive German names chosen
  2026-09-24; Frisée, Escariol and Endiviensalat are kept as synonyms). Broad names such as "Endivien" or "Endiviensalat"
  in shop and garden sources are context only and do not become the crop name.
- Sources that speak only about "Endivie" in general (for example Sperli,
  Kiepenkerl, Bingenheimer, most Hortipendium figures) do not separate the two
  types; their values are used here only where marked and are flagged as
  not Frisée-specific.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: 49 days, counted from transplanting to harvest. Source
  basis: garden and seed-company sources give 10-12 weeks from sowing to
  harvest for Frisée ([Samen.de](https://samen.de/blog/unterschiede-zwischen-eskariol-und-frisee-welche-endivienart-passt-zu-ihrem-garten.html)),
  about 10 weeks for endive in general
  ([Bingenheimer Saatgut](https://blog.bingenheimersaatgut.de/endivien-frischer-herbst-und-wintersalat)),
  and 3-4 weeks of pre-cultivation
  ([Sperli](https://www.sperli.de/anbauexperte/kulturanleitung-endivien/)).
  Derivation: 11 weeks (77 days) from sowing minus 4 weeks (28 days)
  propagation = 49 days. Uncertainty: about +/- 10 days; a named curly variety
  is quoted at 12-14 weeks by one seed company, and autumn plantings run
  longer because of falling temperatures.
- Propagation duration: 28 days. Source basis: 3-4 weeks
  ([Sperli](https://www.sperli.de/anbauexperte/kulturanleitung-endivien/)) and
  4-5 weeks in press pots ([Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Endivien_Erwerbsanbau)).
  Derivation: 4 weeks chosen inside both ranges.
- Harvest window: 14 days (inferred planning value, not a source value). No
  source states a harvest window for Frisée. Basis: whole heads are cut in one
  pass; blanching timing (1-2 weeks before harvest) and the short post-harvest
  shelf life of the curly type limit how long heads stay marketable. Two weeks
  was chosen as the period over which one planting can be cut. Uncertainty:
  high; needs a source or field feedback.
- Sowing windows: summer/main crop sowing 1 May to 15 July, indicative direct
  sowing 15 May to 1 July. Basis: early May to mid July for pre-cultivation
  ([Sperli](https://www.sperli.de/anbauexperte/kulturanleitung-endivien/)),
  from June for ReinSaat curly varieties, June to July in the Bingenheimer
  guide, April to August for Frisée by one garden source, and planting from
  March to mid August in commercial cultivation
  ([Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Endivien_Erwerbsanbau)).
  Derivation: the window shared by most sources was used; earlier and later
  dates depend on protection and bolting-resistant varieties. Autumn crop =
  sowings from mid June to mid July (harvest to November).
- Sowing depth: 1 cm. Sources: 0.5-1 cm
  ([Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Endivien_Erwerbsanbau)), 1 cm and
  "only lightly covered" for ReinSaat curly varieties, 2-3 cm in one garden
  guide. 1 cm sits in the overlap.
- Spacing: 30 cm x 30 cm (0.30 m x 0.30 m), about 11 plants/m². Source basis:
  [ReinSaat](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/EN/salate/endiviensalat/tr_s_fine_mara_ch_re/)
  gives 30 x 30-40 cm for a curly variety and 40-30 x 30 cm for Wallonne;
  Hortipendium gives 33 x 33 cm (8.0 plants/m²) or 38 x 30 cm (8.9 plants/m²)
  for endive in general and notes that curly types can stand closer than smooth
  types. Derivation: the closest value in the ReinSaat range was chosen because
  the curly type is stated to tolerate closer spacing. Wider spacing (about 35 x
  30 cm, 9.5 plants/m²) is a plausible alternative.
- Yield: 3.5 kg/m². Source basis: the fertilization-regulation table for
  Frisée uses a yield level of 350 dt/ha
  ([LKSH, DüV Annex 4 Table 4](https://www.lksh.de/fileadmin/PDFs/Landwirtschaft/Duengung/Stickstoffbedarfswertefuer_Gemuesekulturen_und_Erdbeeren.pdf)).
  Derivation: 350 dt/ha divided by 10 = 3.5 kg/m² (marketable heads). Cross-check:
  one head of about 0.5 kg ([Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Endivien_Erwerbsanbau),
  early harvests, type not separated) times 8-11 plants/m² gives 4-5.5 kg/m², so
  3.5 is on the cautious side. The DüV figure is a planning yield level, not
  a measured trial yield.
- Thousand kernel weight: 1.5 g. Source basis: 1.3-1.6 g for endive in general
  (Hortipendium); ReinSaat gives 1.42 g (Très fine maraîchère), 1.63 g
  (Wallonne) and 1.67 g (Capriccio). Derivation: middle of the range.
- Nutrient demand: medium. Source basis: the DüV table lists 150 kg N/ha at
  350 dt/ha for Frisée, the same value as head lettuce and below eissalat (175)
  and leaf parsley (160) in the same table. No source calling Frisée
  "Mittelzehrer" was read; the classification "medium" is inferred from the N
  value relative to other salads. Uncertainty: moderate.
- Crop family: Asteraceae (Korbblütler), from botanical classification and the
  rotation advice against chicory relatives.
- Field/form constraints: whole days for durations; spacing and depth in
  cm converted to metres only during sync.

## Research Status
- Researched on: 2026-09-24
- General crop note exists: not applicable (this is the general crop note)
- Open source conflicts:
  - Frost: Samen.de states Frisée tolerates about -3 degrees C and Escariol
    about -1 degrees C; Sperli and other garden guides state the opposite
    (smooth types hardier, curly types less frost-resistant); Bingenheimer
    says both tolerate light frost. No trial source found; no frost value is
    set.
  - Spacing: Hortipendium is internally inconsistent (same 8-9 plants/m² for
    both types in the data list, but closer spacing possible for curly types
    in the text); seed companies give 30 x 30-40 cm; garden sources 30 x 35.
  - Sowing depth 0.5-1 cm versus 2-3 cm.
  - Culture duration: 10-12 weeks (garden sources) versus 12-14 weeks (one
    named variety) versus 90-100 days for endive in general.
  - Botanical authority for var. crispum differs between sources; POWO not
    verified.
- Not found: Frisée-specific data from LfL Bayern, Bio Austria, LWG
  Veitshöchheim, DLR, Agroscope/Bioaktuell (searched; no accessible endive
  sheet), so figures rest on the DüV table, Hortipendium and seed companies.
  The 2005 LfL field-vegetable publication has no endive entry.
- Open mapping questions: the split of Endiviensalat into Krause Endivie and Glatte Endivie
  (documented above); botanical rank and naming; harvest window is an inferred
  value; nutrient demand "medium" is inferred; single spacing and no frost
  value chosen; sowing windows are year-dependent and only indicative in the
  model; blanching and bolting have no dedicated field.
- Public-readiness: needs review; the main planning values are secondary
  sources (DüV yield level, Hortipendium, seed companies) and two values are
  inferred.
