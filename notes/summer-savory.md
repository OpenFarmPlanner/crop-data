## Short Description
- Summer savory (`Bohnenkraut`, specifically `Sommerbohnenkraut`) is
  `Satureja hortensis`, an annual culinary herb from the mint family,
  traditionally grown as a companion plant next to beans.
- Identity/mapping note (explicit, not silent): the source for this batch
  only says "Bohnenkraut" without specifying annual summer savory
  (`Sommerbohnenkraut`, `Satureja hortensis`) or perennial winter savory
  (`Winterbohnenkraut`, `Satureja montana`). Per the task instructions, this
  note defaults to `Sommerbohnenkraut` as the more classic annual
  bean-companion herb and the more commonly grown culinary form. The two
  species differ materially: winter savory is perennial, hardier, taller (up
  to about 70 cm vs. about 55 cm), propagated by cuttings/division as well as
  seed, and has a stronger, more resinous flavor
  ([naturadb.de - Satureja montana](https://www.naturadb.de/pflanzen/satureja-montana/)).
  If the intended crop is actually winter savory, this note and its planning
  values do not apply and a separate crop identity should be created.
- Growth duration: 60 days from planting out to first cut; harvest window 90
  days; propagation duration 42 days (see `Planning Value Derivations`).
- Sun-loving, warmth-demanding annual; grown from seed only. Low nutrient
  demand; excess fertilizing reduces aroma.

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: pre-cultivate under glass/protection for an earlier, more
  reliable stand, since seed is fine and slow to establish outdoors.
- Sowing: about March to May under glass.
- Outdoor planting: about 6 weeks (42 days) after sowing, after hardening
  off, once frost risk has passed (early to late May).

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow from late spring once the soil has warmed; keep the
  fine seed only lightly covered and evenly moist until germination.
- Sowing: about May to June outdoors.
- Sowing depth: sow on the surface or only very shallowly covered (no precise
  cm figure found in the sources checked; treated as a surface/very-shallow
  sower like other small-seeded Lamiaceae herbs, but left open rather than
  guessed as a specific number).

- Spacing: about 15-20 cm between plants.
- Site: full sun, permeable, nutrient-rich but not heavily fertilized soil.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest from about July to September, cutting shoots and leaves as needed;
  cutting before flowering gives the best aroma.
- Classic culinary and companion-planting herb for bean dishes and bean beds;
  said to improve bean flavor and deter some bean pests when interplanted.

## Notes
- Traditionally sown or planted alongside beans (`Buschbohne`, `Ackerbohne`)
  as a companion herb; this is a culinary/companion-planting association, not
  a botanical relationship.
- Aroma is strongest when grown lean; avoid overwatering and heavy feeding.

## Planning Value Derivations

- Propagation duration: 42 days. [grove.eco](https://www.grove.eco/en/plants/satureja-hortensis/) recommends pre-growing
  about 42 days before transplanting (mid-March to mid-April sowing, planting
  early to late May). Used as given.
- Growth duration: 60 days, measured from planting out to the first cut. The
  same source words the 60-day growing period loosely ("from sowing") and
  gives first harvest around early July, which matches about 60 days after a
  May planting; the value is therefore interpreted as measured from planting
  out. Uncertainty: direct-sown crops (April
  to June) take about 60 days from sowing to first cut instead, so a
  direct-sown crop is ready earlier relative to its planting date; the value
  is defined for the transplant variant.
- Harvest window: 90 days. Inferred, not a direct source value: the source
  allows cutting from early July to late October (about 110 days), but
  [Floragard](https://www.floragard.de/de-de/pflanzeninfothek/pflanze/kraeuter/satureja-hortensis)
  lists harvest as July to September and aroma is best before flowering, so
  about 3 months (rounded to 90 days) is used as the planning window. A second
  sowing in June or July gives younger, more aromatic shoots.
- Identity caveat: the values apply to annual `Satureja hortensis` only, not
  to perennial winter savory.
