## Short Description
- `Melone` (melon, `Cucumis melo`) is used here as one general crop covering two
  cultivar groups: the netted, aromatic `Cantaloup-Typ` (cantaloupe/musk melon,
  broadly `Cucumis melo` Cantalupensis/Reticulatus groups) and the smooth-,
  waxy-skinned `Honigmelone-Typ` (honeydew and related winter melons, broadly
  `Cucumis melo` Inodorus group).
- Both types are kept as one Kultur rather than split into two, because
  [ReinSaat](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/EN/melone/zuckermelone_honigmelone/)
  groups both under a single shop category, "Sugar melons/Honey melons", and no
  variety name was given for either type in this batch that would justify a
  separate, more specific crop identity. See `Notes` for the type differences
  that are still documented explicitly.
- Growth duration: 84 days from planting out to first ripe fruit; harvest
  window 28 days; propagation duration 35 days. These are representative
  planning values across both types (see `Planning Value Derivations`).

## Sowing & Planting

### Transplants
- Description: pre-cultivate indoors because melons are cold-sensitive; one
  seed per pot to avoid root disturbance at transplanting.
- Sowing: early to end of April indoors.
- Outdoor planting: not before late May to June, once frost risk has passed
  and both air and soil are reliably warm.

### Direct Sowing
- Description: possible outdoors in warm regions/seasons once soil is warm
  enough, but pre-cultivation is the more commonly documented path for
  Central European climates because of the crop's warmth requirement.
- Sowing: from early May outdoors at the earliest, only after soil has warmed.
- Sowing depth: 1-2 cm.

*(Overwintering is not applicable; melon is a warm-season annual crop.)*

- Spacing: sources disagree on a single figure. General guidance ranges from
  about 60-80 cm between plants up to 150 cm row spacing with 45-100 cm within
  the row, and a simpler rule of at least 1 m² per plant. Documented as an open
  source conflict rather than picking one figure silently (see `Planning Value
  Derivations`).
- Site: sunny, warm, wind-protected location; loose, humus-rich, quickly
  warming soil. Heavy feeder (`Starkzehrer`); benefits from compost
  incorporation before planting and additional feeding during flowering and
  fruit set.

## Harvest & Use
- Cantaloup-Typ: typically detaches ("slips") from the stem at full ripeness
  and develops a strong, sweet aroma; harvest window from late summer into
  autumn.
- Honigmelone-Typ: usually stays attached to the vine at ripeness and has a
  much fainter aroma than cantaloupe types; ripeness is judged more by skin
  colour change and a dull, hollow sound when tapped, and fruit generally
  stores longer after harvest.
- Both types: harvest roughly from late August into autumn depending on sowing
  date and local climate.

## Notes
- Botanical/type distinction: `Cantaloup-Typ` and `Honigmelone-Typ` are
  cultivar groups within one species, `Cucumis melo`, not separate species.
  ReinSaat's own catalogue keeps them in one shop category, which supports
  treating them as type notes inside a single general crop record rather than
  as separate Kulturen.
- High nutrient demand throughout the season; sensitive to cold, especially at
  germination and immediately after transplanting.
- Susceptible to powdery mildew and aphids in warm, dry conditions; good air
  circulation and crop rotation are commonly recommended.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Propagation duration: 35 days. [Lubera](https://www.lubera.com/de/gartenbuch/melonen-pflanzen-p5150) gives about 4-5 weeks from
  sowing until seedlings are ready to plant out (sowing mid-April for a late
  May/June planting); [Plantura](https://www.plantura.garden/gemuese/melonen/melonen-pflanzen) places indoor sowing in April with
  outdoor planting from end of May to June. Used: 5 weeks = 35 days.
- Growth duration: 84 days, measured from planting out to the first ripe
  fruit. Inferred, not a direct source value: planting from late May to early
  June and first harvest from late August (both sources) means about 11-13
  weeks; 12 weeks is used. Other garden guidance cites 80-100 days from
  sowing, which would be much shorter after planting; that figure seems to
  describe early types or protected cultivation and is not used.
  `Cantaloup-Typ` melons ripen earlier (lower end, about 70-80 days) and
  `Honigmelone-Typ` types later (upper end and beyond, about 90-100 days);
  84 days is a mid-season compromise for open-field or tunnel cultivation in
  Central Europe. Uncertainty: about +/- 14 days depending on type, summer
  weather and variety.
- Harvest window: 28 days. Inferred: sources give harvest from late August
  "into autumn" and 6-8 fruits per plant that ripen one after another, but no
  window in days. Four weeks is used as a plausible per-planting window;
  late honeydew types and cold autumns can shorten it because harvest must
  end before frost.
- Spacing: sources give a wide, partly conflicting range - about 60-80 cm
  between plants
  ([einrichtungsbeispiele.de](https://www.einrichtungsbeispiele.de/teich-pflanzen/diverse-biotope/cucumis-melo-slnk.html)),
  1-1.5 m between plants (general guidance), and 150 x 45-100 cm row x in-row
  spacing (general guidance), versus a simpler "at least 1 m² per plant" rule
  from [Plantura](https://www.plantura.garden/gemuese/melonen/melonen-pflanzen).
  Kept as an open range rather than a single invented figure; documented as an
  open source conflict.
- Yield: no reliable general kg/m² figure was found for either type; left
  open rather than estimated.
