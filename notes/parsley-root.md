## Short Description
- Parsley root (`Wurzelpetersilie`, synonym `Hamburg parsley`; German also
  `Petersilienwurzel`) is `Petroselinum crispum` subsp. `tuberosum`, grown for
  the thickened, parsnip-like taproot. The identity is the harvested root
  (storage crop, soup greens). The leaves are edible but are only a by-product;
  leaf types are separate Kulturen (curly and flat-leaf parsley). The plain
  names "Petersilie" and "Parsley" are deliberately not used as synonyms here
  because they denote the leaf crops.
- Biennial grown as an annual, Apiaceae. Frost-hardy: foliage dies back at
  about -7 to -10 degrees C while roots survive colder, so roots can stay in the
  ground over winter. Slow, irregular germination. Nutrient demand `medium`
  (see `Planning Value Derivations`); potassium-hungry.
- Growth duration: 180 days from sowing (direct sown; planning value; source
  ranges 170-180 days and 180-210 days).

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: sow directly in loose, deep, humus-rich, well-draining soil;
  transplanting is not usual for a taproot crop. Germination is slow: per
  [Hortipendium](https://www.hortipendium.de/Petersilie) about 45 days at 5
  degrees C, 25 days at 12 degrees C and 12 days at 20 degrees C. Avoid
  high-salt fertilizer near the seed row. Commercial cultivation can use raised
  beds and band sowing.
- Sowing: March to April (main window; [ReinSaat](https://www.reinsaat.at/shop/DE/wurzelpetersilie_1/),
  Hortipendium). Mid to late April for winter harvest (ReinSaat); garden
  sources extend to June for an earlier, smaller crop.
- Sowing depth: 1-2 cm (Hortipendium, garden seed shop); ReinSaat says shallow,
  up to about 1 cm. Planning value 1 cm.

- Spacing: 30 cm between rows; 3 cm within the row after thinning (ReinSaat
  30-50 cm x 2 cm; garden sources 30-40 cm x 2-3 cm; Hortipendium about 75 cm
  bed spacing with band sowing and 30-40 plants/m²).
- Site: sun, deep loose humus-rich soil, even moisture.

## Harvest & Use
- Harvest from about October to December when roots are fully developed; roots
  are frost-hardy and may be lifted as needed (field storage) or lifted before
  hard frost and stored. Per [Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wurzelpetersilie)
  unwashed roots keep below 2 degrees C like carrots and up to about six months
  in moist sand (clamp storage). Earlier sowings are sold from July as bunched
  roots.
- Use: soup greens, stews, grated raw; leaves as seasoning, taken sparingly so
  root growth is not reduced.

## Notes
- Rotation: at least 4 years break after carrot, parsnip, celery and other
  Apiaceae (Hortipendium).
- Thousand kernel weight: about 1-1.5 g (ReinSaat).
- Seed requirement (ReinSaat): about 30-40 g per are; 50-60 seeds per row meter.
- Common carrot-fly and Apiaceae disease pressure applies; keep rotation.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Growth duration: 180 days from sowing. Sources: 170-180 days (garden seed
  shops) and 180-210 days (Hortipendium). The overlap point 180 is used;
  later harvests simply extend the harvest window. Uncertainty about +/- 20
  days by sowing date and harvest date.
- Harvest window: 60 days, inferred. Sources give a harvest from about October
  to November/December, and roots can stay in the ground. About two months
  from earliest usable size is used; not a direct source value.
- Sowing depth: 1 cm, lower bound of the 1-2 cm range, consistent with
  ReinSaat's "up to about 1 cm"; rounded to a whole centimeter.
- Spacing: 30 cm x 3 cm; ReinSaat and garden sources overlap here. About 110
  plants/m² is far above Hortipendium's 30-40 plants/m² for large commercial
  roots; a mismatch, see Research Status.
- Yield: 4.0 kg/m² (`per_sqm`, root without foliage). Hortipendium gives about
  400 dt/ha = 4.0 kg/m². A garden source (search-result summary only, not
  verified on the page) mentions 1-1.6 kg/m². The commercial value is used
  as a planning value, garden yields may be far lower.
- Nutrient demand: `medium`. A garden seed shop calls it a moderate feeder;
  Hortipendium stresses above-average potassium but no N-based class.
  Classification is not consistently source-backed.
- Thousand kernel weight: 1.2 g, rounded value inside the ReinSaat 1-1.5 g
  range.
- Germination: slow, 12-45 days depending on soil temperature (Hortipendium);
  Wikipedia gives 15-20 days.
