## Short Description
- Bush bean (`Buschbohne`) is the dwarf form of `Phaseolus vulgaris`, grown
  here for green pods (distinct from the dry-seed `Kidneybohne` use).
- Warm-season, frost-sensitive annual legume; fixes nitrogen, so nutrient
  demand is low to moderate. Even moisture during flowering and pod set.
- Growth duration is short: about 7-9 weeks from sowing to first green pods.

## Sowing & Planting

### Direct Sowing
- Description: direct sow into warm soil (about 12-15 C) after the last frost;
  beans dislike root disturbance, so pre-cultivation is unusual.
- Sowing: about mid May to early July, in successions.
- Sowing depth: 3-4 cm.

- Spacing: about 40 cm between rows, in clusters or every few cm in the row.
- Site: full sun, warm, sheltered, well-drained soil.

## Harvest & Use
- Pick pods young and tender, repeatedly over a few weeks; regular picking
  keeps the plant producing.

## Comparison With General Crop Data
- The live general record already carries growth duration 49 days, harvest
  window 21 days, and yield about 1.3 kg/m² (`per_sqm`). Only the thousand
  kernel weight was missing.
- Thousand kernel weight: 160 g, matching the live variety record `Faraday`
  (160.72 g); green bush-bean seed is far lighter than large kidney-bean seed.

## Planning Value Derivations
- Thousand kernel weight: 160 g as a planning value for a green bush bean.
  `Phaseolus vulgaris` seed spans about 250-530 g per thousand across all types
  ([pflanzen-deutschland](https://www.pflanzen-deutschland.de/Phaseolus_vulgaris.html)),
  but green bush types sit well below that; the project's `Faraday` record
  measures 160.72 g, so 160 g is used for the general crop.
- Propagation duration: left empty. Bush beans are direct sown.
