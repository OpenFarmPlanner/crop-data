# Crop Species Names: French And Spanish

Reference list of French (`fr`) and Spanish (`es`) names for every published
official crop species ("Kulturart") in the OpenFarmPlanner crop library. It is
the Phase 1 research basis for adding `fr` and `es` translations to the species
records through the crop-species API (see
[`crop-species-api.md`](crop-species-api.md)).

The file contains no IDs, hosts, or tokens. In Phase 2, rows are matched to
live records by `scientific_name` plus the German common name.

## Snapshot

- Read on 2026-10-20 from the published species list: **202 species**, all
  with `de` and `en` translations, **none with `fr` or `es`**.
- The planner accepts `fr` and `es` as translation language codes
  (`SUPPORTED_LANGUAGE_CODES` in the planner's `backend/config/languages.py`);
  no test write was made.
- Only 13 of the 202 records had a live `scientific_name` (15 after the 2026-10-20 changes to Gelbsenf and Bischofskraut). For the others the
  `scientific_name` column shows `— (ref. …)`: the botanical name used here to
  pin down the identity before translating. It is a research reference, not a
  value to write.

## Naming Rules Applied

- **Same identity as `de`.** Specific crops keep specific names (Buschbohne →
  Haricot nain / Judía enana; Einlegegurke → Cornichon / Pepinillo, not
  Concombre / Pepino). A specific species is never mapped to an umbrella term.
- **Canonical = the usual name in France (`fr`) or Spain (`es`)**, analogous to
  "Bundesdeutsch is canonical" for `de`. Belgian, Swiss, Canadian or Latin
  American variants (for example Rampon, Bleuet for blueberry, Frijol, Ejote,
  Jitomate, Chile, Papa) appear only as synonyms.
- **No `regional_names` for `fr`/`es`.** The only allowed keys are `austria`
  and `switzerland`, and regions for French or Spanish are not modelled.
- **Ambiguous terms are synonyms on every species they can mean, never a common
  name** (the "Peperoni" rule from the planner's
  [crop taxonomy guidelines](https://github.com/OpenFarmPlanner/planner/blob/main/docs/crop-taxonomy-guidelines.md)
  §4). Examples: Haricot vert / Judía verde (all green-bean records),
  Chou frisé (kale and Savoy), Guindilla (chili and Pfefferoni), Colinabo
  (kohlrabi and swede), Fenouil / Hinojo (both fennel records).
- **Bare umbrella words are not added as aliases** where they would match many
  species (Pois, Haricot, Judía, Col, Chou), mirroring the absence of a plain
  `Bohne` or `Kohl` in `de`.
- **Fruit crops** follow the existing `en` pattern: the fruit name is the common
  name (Pomme, Manzana) and the tree name a synonym (Pommier, Manzano).
- **Spelling** follows the `en` entries: first letter capitalised, the rest per
  language rules, singular, no article.
- `en` entries were only reviewed, not changed; see
  [en: observations](#en-observations).

## Sources And How They Were Used

Names were checked by scientific name and use form; source text was not
copied. Sources are used in this order:

1. Official multilingual references by scientific name:
   [UPOV GENIE](https://www.upov.int/genie/en/) (the plant-variety
   protection database of UPOV, with French and Spanish common names per
   taxon), the [EPPO Global Database](https://gd.eppo.int/), and the
   [CPVO](https://cpvo.europa.eu/) technical protocols.
2. Official market and statistics texts: the EU marketing standards
   ([Regulation 543/2011](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32011R0543)),
   the [Interfel](https://www.interfel.com/) tomato segmentation, the
   [Agreste](https://agreste.agriculture.gouv.fr/) regional yearbooks
   (France) and the
   [MAPA statistical yearbook](https://servicio.mapa.gob.es/estadistica/pags/anuario/2001/AE_2001-Anx_14.pdf)
   (Spain).
3. Seed catalogues from the reference country: [Biau Germe](https://biaugerme.com/),
   [Germinance](https://www.germinance.com/),
   [La Ferme de Sainte Marthe](https://lafermedesaintemarthe.com/) (France);
   [Semillas Batlle](https://semillasbatlle.com/), [Fitó](https://www.fitoseeds.com/)
   and the seed and plant range of [Planeta Huerto](https://www.planetahuerto.es/)
   (Spain).
4. Technical and consumer texts: MAPA library articles, the
   [Cajamar horticulture bulletin](https://www.cajamar.es/storage/documents/boletin-huerto-79-1496056884-11997.pdf),
   [DIY Seeds](https://www.diyseeds.org/) crop sheets, [OCU](https://www.ocu.org/),
   [Mètode](https://metode.es/) (University of Valencia).
5. Dictionary check of everyday names: [Larousse](https://www.larousse.fr/)
   (French dictionary and Spanish–French dictionary), counted only when the
   definition names the crop meant here (Larousse "amidonnier" means a starch
   maker and was not counted for emmer).
6. Botanical anchor: [GRIN Taxonomy](https://npgsweb.ars-grin.gov/gringlobal/taxon/taxonomysearch),
   linked per row where available.

Wikidata labels were used as a cross-check only. Automatic matches against
catalogue URLs were spot-checked, and matches inside unrelated products (for
example "tomate cerise" for Kirsche, or cosmetics and snacks in a general
garden shop) were discarded. Official names were preferred over catalogue
names where they differ, unless the official name is ambiguous in the
reference country.

## Open questions

### Identity questions (species unclear)

- **Schnittkohl** (fr Chou à faucher / es Nabicol): Identity conflict: the live scientific name is Brassica oleracea var. viridis (planner bootstrap key leaf_cabbage, UPOV collard group: fr Chou cavalier / Chou fourrager, es Col forrajera), but notes/siberian-kale.md and crop-species-linking.md use Brassica napus var. pabularia. The names given follow the note (UPOV GENIE and the CPVO protocol: Chou à faucher / Chou sibérien, es Nabicol). Resolve the identity first.
- **Winterkresse** (fr Cresson de terre / es Hierba de Santa Bárbara): Species open in crop-species-linking.md (Barbarea verna vs. B. vulgaris). Hierba de Santa Bárbara (UPOV GENIE for B. verna) fits both Barbarea species. Berro de tierra and fr Cresson de jardin are also used for garden cress (Lepidium), so they are synonyms only.

### Duplicate suspects

- **Rucola** (fr Roquette / es Rúcula): Duplicate suspect: the library also has Salatrauke (Eruca) and Wilde Rauke (Diplotaxis); Rucola can mean either. By the Peperoni rule Roquette/Rúcula should be search aliases on both, not the common name of a third record. Resolve the duplicate first.
- **Salatrauke** (fr Roquette cultivée / es Rúcula): See Rucola. es has no everyday qualifier for Eruca; Rúcula is the normal Spain name.

### No unambiguous name

- **Strohblume** (fr Immortelle à bractées / es Perpetua): fr Immortelle à bractées per UPOV GENIE (bare Immortelle also names Helichrysum italicum). es: UPOV GENIE gives Perpetua and Siempreviva, but both also name other everlasting flowers in Spain (Helichrysum stoechas, Limonium, Gomphrena), so no unambiguous everyday name was found.

### No established name in France or Spain

- **Fleischtomate** (fr Tomate à côtes / es Tomate asurcado): Closest official names are the EU commercial type ribbed tomato (fr à côtes, es asurcado), but Interfel files heart-shaped (oxheart) tomatoes under allongée, while the de note includes oxheart in Fleischtomate. No single France or Spain name covers the de record. Taxonomy note: likely a variety-level distinction of Tomate.
- **Kohlröschen** (fr Kalette / es Kalette): New hybrid; only the brand names Kalettes / Flower Sprout circulate (in Spain sold as Kalettes, per Infoagro trade news). No generic fr or es name found; the de canonical name was itself chosen to avoid the brand.
- **Pfefferoni** (fr Peperoncini / es Piparra): No France or Spain equivalent of the Austrian Pfefferoni category (pointed mild-to-hot Capsicum annuum, see notes/pepper-pfefferoni.md). es Piparra is the Basque pickled pepper (closest match, regional); Guindilla is shared with Chili; fr imports the Italian Peperoncini.
- **Physalis** (fr Physalis / es Physalis): es: no settled Spain name besides the Latin loan; Spanish seed packets use Physalis with Uchuva (Rocalba). Alquequenje names the ornamental P. alkekengi and is deliberately not used. fr Physalis: Larousse ties the word to the ornamental alkekengi.
- **Puntarelle** (fr Chicorée catalogne / es Puntarelle): es: no Spain name found; the Italian name is used in trade.

### Several equally common names

- **Emmer** (fr Amidonnier / es Farro): fr Amidonnier per UPOV GENIE (spelled amidonier there). es: no settled Spain name; UPOV GENIE gives Escaña almidonera (garbled as esaña almidora), retail uses Farro, and escanda/escaña overlap with spelt and einkorn.
- **Frühlingszwiebel** (fr Oignon nouveau / es Cebolleta): The German record mixes Allium cepa spring onions and A. fistulosum (synonym Winterzwiebel). es Cebolleta is the UPOV GENIE name for A. fistulosum and is used for spring onions generally. fr has no single name: Ciboule is A. fistulosum (UPOV GENIE), Oignon nouveau / Oignon blanc are A. cepa bunching onions.
- **Schlangengurke** (fr Concombre long / es Pepino largo): es: Pepino largo, Pepino holandés and Pepino tipo Almería are all used in Spain.
- **Stängelkohl** (fr Brocoli-rave / es Grelo): fr: no settled France name; Rapini (Canada) and the Italian Cima di rapa also circulate. es: Grelo is the Galician turnip-top crop of Brassica rapa (Semillas Batlle sells nabo grelos); confirm it matches the de use form.

## en: observations

The `en` entries were reviewed but not changed. Points worth a separate
decision:

- **Schnittkohl** — `en` is "Cutting cabbage", an uncommon term;
  [`crop-species-linking.md`](crop-species-linking.md) recommends "Siberian
  kale". The live `scientific_name` is *Brassica oleracea* var. *viridis*, while
  `notes/siberian-kale.md` and the linking doc use *Brassica napus* var.
  *pabularia*. This is an identity conflict, not only a naming issue.
- **Kohlröschen** — `en` is "Kalettes", a trademark. The linking doc notes that
  no canonical English name has been decided.
- **Chicorée** — `en` is "Chicory", which in English covers all *Cichorium
  intybus* forms (radicchio, sugarloaf, puntarelle). "Belgian endive" or
  "Witloof chicory" would match the `de` identity.
- **Rettich** — `en` is "Daikon radish", narrower than `de`, whose synonyms
  include Winterrettich and Radi (black and other winter radishes). "Winter
  radish" would match.
- **Ribisel** — `en` is "Currant"; the `de` record is red currant and
  blackcurrant is a separate species. "Redcurrant" would be precise.
- **Kürbis** — `en` is "Pumpkin"; the `de` record covers winter squash in
  general. "Winter squash" may fit better.
- **Zierkürbis** — `en` is "Ornamental pumpkin"; the usual term is
  "Ornamental gourd".
- **Raps** — `en` is "Canola", the North American trade name; "Oilseed rape"
  is the usual British and international agronomic term.
- **Winterkresse** — `en` is "Winter cress" (usually *Barbarea vulgaris*); the
  linking doc recommends "Land cress" (*B. verna*). Tied to the open species
  question.
- **Rucola / Salatrauke** — `en` "Rocket" and "Garden rocket" are synonyms of
  each other in English, which supports the duplicate suspicion.
- **Senf → Gelbsenf** — resolved on 2026-10-20: the record was renamed and its
  `en` name changed from the umbrella "Mustard" to "White mustard" (with
  "Mustard" as a synonym).
- Minor: Bärlauch "Ramson" is usually "Ramsons" or "Wild garlic"; Edelkastanie
  "Chestnut" could be "Sweet chestnut"; Kiwi could be "Kiwifruit".

## Taxonomy remarks outside this task

Translating exposed records that may conflict with the planner's crop taxonomy
guidelines. They were translated as they are, without changing their identity:

- Fleischtomate, Kirschtomate, Salattomate and Soßentomate look like
  variety-level distinctions of Tomate (guidelines §3).
- Salat is an umbrella record next to Kopfsalat, Eisbergsalat and Römersalat.
- Petersilie exists next to Krause and Glatte Petersilie; Gurke next to
  Schlangengurke and Einlegegurke; Grüne Bohne next to Buschbohne and
  Stangenbohne.
- Rucola and Salatrauke look like duplicates (see Open questions).
